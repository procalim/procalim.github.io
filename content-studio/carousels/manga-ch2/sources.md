# Sources for the slide facts (checked 2026-11-09)

Checked through web search results. The pages themselves (pure.psu.edu, www.sf.mpg.de) could not be opened from this
session, so the wording below follows the search summaries of those pages.

- Law 1, slide 3: Rolls BJ, Rolls ET, Rowe EA & Sweeney K, "Sensory specific satiety in man", Physiology & Behavior
  27(1):137-142 (1981). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7267792/
  - 32 people rated the taste of eight foods, ate one of them for lunch, then rated all eight again.
  - Liking for the food they ate fell more than for the foods they did not eat.
  - "Oxford" on the slide: the Society for the Study of Ingestive Behavior's "Ingestive Classics" page says the team
    began these studies at Oxford, and the group's 1982 paper carries the address Department of Experimental
    Psychology, University of Oxford. The 1981 paper's own address line was not seen.
- Law 2, slide 4: Rolls BJ, Van Duijvenvoorde PM & Rolls ET, "Pleasantness changes and food intake in a varied
  four-course meal", Appetite 5(4):337-348 (1984).
  https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/pleasantness-changes-and-food-intake-in-a-varied-four-course-meal/
  - 48 people ate a four-course lunch: a different food in each course, or the same food in every course.
  - The varied meal raised intake by about 60%. The slide says "about 60%".
- Law 3, slide 5: Minère M, Wilhelms H, Kuzmanovic B and colleagues (group of Henning Fenselau, Max Planck Institute
  for Metabolism Research, Cologne), Science, February 2025, DOI 10.1126/science.adp1510.
  https://www.sf.mpg.de/2103868/250212_dessert_stomach
  - In mice: the POMC neurons that signal fullness also release an opioid (beta-endorphin) when a full mouse meets
    sugar. Blocking that pathway stopped full mice from eating more sugar and did nothing in hungry mice.
  - In people the evidence is small: the same brain region reacted to a sugar solution during a scan. The slide
    therefore says "in mice" and makes no claim about people.
- Not used: Wansink's studies (retractions), and any exact number for how much "room" dessert adds.
- The dessert in the panels is the Crinkle Cake, recipe 113 in the book (`src/data/recipes.ts`, slug `crinkle-cake`).
  The main course in panel 3 is drawn as plain roast beef and no slide names it.
