# Sources for the slide facts (checked 2026-11-09)
- Eyes: Michel, Velasco, Gatti & Spence, "A taste of Kandinsky", Flavour 3:7 (2014), Oxford Crossmodal Research
  Laboratory. The same salad was served in 3 presentations; the art-inspired one was rated tastier after eating, and
  people would pay more for it. No percentage is used on the slide (secondary reports disagree).
- Nose: Spence, "Just how much of what we taste derives from the sense of smell?", Flavour 4:30 (2015). Smell is a
  large part of flavour (retronasal smell; a pinched nose weakens it). There is no solid percentage, so "80%" is not
  used.
- Ears: Zampini & Spence, "The role of auditory cues in modulating the perceived crispness and staleness of potato
  chips", Journal of Sensory Studies 19:347-363 (2004), Oxford; Ig Nobel 2008. A louder crunch, or more of its high
  frequencies, made the same chips feel crisper and fresher.
- Not used: the plate-colour study (strawberry mousse rated sweeter on a white plate). The comic serves on a black
  tray, so it would contradict the story.
