Two kinds of reel:
- **Kitchen reels** (a real recipe for a real problem: the kids, the oven, the temperature): more people skip (45-49%),
  and the ones who stay save 11-17 per 1,000 and follow. These reach cooks, the people who buy a cookbook.
- **Show reels** (a spectacle: the lantern, the ring box, the ice cubes, the safe): fewer skip and they watch longer,
  and almost nobody saves, sends or follows (0-1.6 per 1,000). These reach scrollers.
- **The Crinkle Cake was both**: a show to stop on and a recipe to make, which is why it went to 380K and brought
  the follower days.

95% of views from non-followers is normal: Instagram tests every reel on strangers. Which strangers come next is
decided by what the first ones did. Saves and sends from the first viewers teach Instagram "cooks"; watching without
saving teaches it "entertainment".

The retention slide is the same shape on every reel: half the viewers gone by about 6 s, the account's average watch
6-12 s. The 3 a.m. Crunchwrap is the best of all 54 on both counts (skip 22.2%, watch 15.5 s): the Genjutsu copy holds
people. What it lacks is the kitchen reason to save (1.3 a 1,000 against the patties' 17).
