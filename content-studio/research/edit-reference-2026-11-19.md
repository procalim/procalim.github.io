## What all six do
1. **The result first, about 3 s.** Five open on the finished food (or, for both salads, the moment the whole dish is
   visible: the dressing poured over the full sectioned bowl); the finished food stays 2.4-4.6 s (median 3.3 s), then
   the recipe starts from its first step. The opening holds one texture action: torn open, bitten open, poured over.
2. **The hook is on screen at frame one.** All six show their text at 0.0 s, already complete (no typing in), and keep
   it through the whole result section; three keep a small label the whole reel.
3. **The hook is the dish's name with a twist**: "(no protein powder)", "3 ingredients only", "HEALTHY …", or a
   routine line ("This is your sign…"). Short: 2-8 words. Our own account says a plain name alone skips most (47.4%,
   research/hook-bank.md), so ours keeps the hook bank's question or number and adds the dish.
4. **Position**: centre-low (62-67% of the height) in four, top third in two; never at the very top or bottom.
5. **After the opening, the steps go fast**: 0.54-1.35 cuts a second in the four short reels (median 0.83), one
   ingredient or one action per cut. Our own 68 reels say more cuts = more skip (0.2-0.35 a second in the August
   winners), so ours keeps the calm shots and lets each ingredient land inside them instead of adding cuts.

## How our edits use it (from the step-by-step salad, 2026-11-19)
- 0-3.5 s: the take's last shot (the result) moved to the opening, one shot, no cut; the hook complete at frame one,
  centre-low over the food's top edge, kept the whole 3.5 s; no typing on the hook (the re-hooks stay typed).
- Then every step in order, one short card per step (the ingredient count, the dressing's ingredients, the protein,
  the fridge time), no gap over 2.5 s; then the result again with the answer and the send cards.
