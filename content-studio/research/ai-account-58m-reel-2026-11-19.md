## The 58M reel, measured (the owner's file, 30.9 s)
- **Format:** 720x1280 at 24 fps, the same format as our Seedance takes. No upscale and no 60 fps.
- **Frame one (0.0 s):** hands push dry spaghetti through a raw hot dog. There is no text at any point.
- **Sound:**
  - no voice and no music: spectral flatness median 0.44, speech-band share 0.33;
  - the food's own sound at -15.9 LUFS, with a 16.8 dB range;
  - 8 sharp onsets in 0-3 s (open3.py).
- **Cuts and face:** 23 cuts in 30.9 s, one every 1.3 s on average. The chef's face first shows at 5.3 s.
- **Reveals:**
  - the spaghetti cooked through the hot dog (about 8 s);
  - the rolled log and its spiral (12-13 s);
  - the cheese pull (about 21 s);
  - the knife through the spiral (24-27 s);
  - the bite (about 28 s).

## What it shows
- **The gap is one hit, not the everyday reels.** The hit is the idea: a food everyone knows has something done to it that nobody has
  seen, and it shows in frame one, so the viewer stays to see what it becomes.
- **What did not make the difference:**
  - Picture quality: the same 720p at 24 fps.
  - The AI label: it sits in the bio.
  - The polish of the edit.
- **Silence is not enough on its own.** Our own reel with no text, Pan Chocolate Cake v2 (12 Nov), skipped 62%.

## Proposed (not decided by the owner yet)
- **The new first question in idea-lab:** does frame one show an act that most people have never seen done to a food they know?
  If not, the recipe goes to stories, carousels or DMs, not to a reel.
- **For these recipes:**
  - open on the strange act, because it is the most unusual frame in the reel;
  - run 25-31 s, with 2-3 reveals and the bite last.
- **Result-first** stays for recipes whose steps are ordinary (the salad).
