# Eleven Black Trays Target Real Problems

Make these eleven, one a day from 14 to 24 November 2026: a chicken-crust grid pizza for gym women, a savoury spinach-lentil crinkle for low iron ("tea after it is the mistake"), a Greek-yogurt berry bark for dieters, cottage-cheese pinwheels for PCOS, banana brûlée protein oats for after training, fattoush fins for blood sugar, a stand-up sweet potato tray for picky kids, carrot-courgette roses with garlic oil for IBS, a kofta-lentil grid for pregnancy ("liver is the mistake"), an oat-seed snap slab for high cholesterol and an okra honeycomb for blood pressure. Four of them carry 23-54 g protein a serving, so the gym requirement is met more than twice. The order puts the biggest audiences (protein, weight, PCOS, blood sugar) and the strongest popularity proof first: 167M views for yogurt clusters, 22.6M for chicken-crust pizza, 11M for baked cottage cheese, 15M for the original crinkle cake and 142.7x the median for this account's own crinkle reel. It leaves the low-demand conditions to the end and uses them as "send this to your dad" shares, which is the metric this account still lacks. The hooks with the strongest science are also the most cultural: tea after meals, liver for iron, salt, and garlic. Four caveats apply to everything below. None of the eleven recipes has been test-cooked. Every macro is an estimate built from USDA values recalled from memory. Almost every external number comes from search-result summaries, not fully read sources. One pick, the savoury crinkle, deliberately reworks a posted shape, and a fallback is given for it.

**How to read the flags.** **[S]** means the research note says the figure came from a search-result summary or excerpt, not a fully read page. **[S?]** means the note does not say whether the page was opened, so treat the figure as unverified. **[R]** is the researcher's recall, not re-checked in the session. **[E]** is an estimate: every macro is one, because USDA FoodData Central was blocked and the per-100 g values were recalled. **[A]** is this account's own measured insight from the [reels-director skill file](../.claude/skills/reels-director/SKILL.md). **[U]** means the recipe is untested. No note records a fully read primary source for any figure, so every external number below carries [S] or [S?] and needs a check before it goes on screen.

## Demand favours protein, weight and PCOS, while tea reaches everyone

The region's largest problems for women aged 18-34 are weight and hormones, not cholesterol. Adult female obesity is about **48.5% in Saudi Arabia, 49% in Iraq and about 59% in Egypt** (the Egypt figure needs verifying) [S] ([World Obesity](https://data.worldobesity.org/tables/ranking-obesity-by-country-adults-1.pdf)). Jordan has **43.1% obesity among women of reproductive age** [S?] ([PMC11783968](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11783968/)). The gym audience is real and growing: **44.6% of Saudi women** meet the 150-minute activity guideline [S?] ([GASTAT](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/20117/2435273/Saudi+Women+Statistics+2024_EN+%281%29.pdf/9aa5a79c-cea5-9239-bd9f-ece710b6c33d)), and women are forecast to be the fastest-growing gym segment at **13.05% a year** (a vendor forecast) [S?] ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-health-and-fitness-club-market)). PCOS affects **about 16% of women in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait** in general-population studies, with a GCC range of 4.6-27.6% [S?] ([PMC13660512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13660512)), and #PCOS has **5.7bn TikTok views** [S?] ([Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-au/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-tiktok-misinformation-cure)). Those three audiences, plus the protein niche's proven USD 29.99-44.95 macro-labelled ebooks [S?] ([Jalal](https://jalalsamfit.com/products/bundle-jalals-cookbook-mealprep-cookbook)), fill four of the first five slots. The iron crinkle takes the other one, for the reason given below.

Blood sugar ranks next because it serves two readers at once. Diabetes affects **23.1% of Saudi adults and 22.4% of Egyptian adults** (13.2M people in Egypt) [S?] ([IDF Saudi Arabia](https://idf.org/our-network/regions-and-members/middle-east-and-north-africa/members/saudi-arabia/); [IDF Egypt](https://idf.org/our-network/regions-and-members/middle-east-and-north-africa/members/egypt/)), and **62-64% of cases in Egypt and the UAE are undiagnosed** [S?] ([IDF Atlas UAE](https://diabetesatlas.org/data-by-location/country/united-arab-emirates/)). A young woman with insulin resistance saves the fattoush for herself and sends it to her father. The same logic justifies keeping cholesterol and blood pressure in the set despite their older skew. Hypertension awareness in Saudi Arabia is only **34.8%** [S?] ([PMC12930371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12930371/)), and this account's Honeycomb Pasta Pie had strong retention (39% to the end) but **zero shares** [A]. A recipe built for a parent is a recipe built to be forwarded.

Anaemia has a weak online identity but affects **28.5-43.7% of women of reproductive age** across Egypt, Iraq, Jordan and Saudi Arabia (2019 modelled values) [S?] ([PMC12887953](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12887953/)). The tea hook gets around the weak identity. The viewer does not need to know she is anaemic to stop on "tea after it is the mistake", because tea after meals is close to universal in the region. That is why the iron crinkle ranks second. Gut health is the fastest-rising niche: "gut health" searches rose **123% in three months** on a US tracker [S?] ([Rising Trends](https://www.risingtrends.co/trends/nutrition-trends-2026)), and IBS meets Rome IV criteria in **20% of Saudi women** in pooled data [S?] ([PMC11966369](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11966369/)). No Arabic keyword or Google Trends data was found for any of these terms, so test the Arabic title words before posting.

## Nine candidates lost on duplication, weak evidence or repeated shapes

Three candidates repeat posted dishes outright. The **protein crinkle bake** reuses the original crinkle's own mistake (pre-baking the phyllo before the custard). The **honeycomb protein rigatoni** is the posted Honeycomb Pasta Pie. The **crispy chickpea chocolate bark**'s mistake ("oiling wet chickpeas") restates the posted «الحمص المبلول هو الغلطة» [A]. Three more are near-duplicates of a stronger pick. The **cottage-cheese spiral bake** is the pinwheels with a weakly sourced mistake ("rolling it hot"). The **protein granola snap slab** competes with the oat-seed slab, and its mistake ("don't stir") comes from a recipe blog ([Food52](https://food52.com/blog/16592-for-clumps-in-your-granola-just-add-water)) where the slab's comes from LDL trials. The **zucchini rose bake** repeats the IBS roses, and even its own sources disagree on salting ([Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/1956143/foolproof-way-saute-zucchini-texture-crispy)).

The last three lost on evidence. **Chilled accordion potatoes** rest on a cooling effect that "may vary from 0-50%" by potato variety [S?] ([Food & Function 2011](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2011/fo/c1fo10042d)), they come straight after the posted potato nests, and their shape duplicates the sweet potato tray. The **mujadara grid** has the weakest mistake in the pool: cooled and reheated rice gave an **18% lower glucose response in 15 healthy adults** [S] ([PubMed 26693746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26693746/)). In type 1 diabetes, cooled rice produced more low-sugar episodes [S?] ([PMC9013350](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9013350/)), and no rice-cooling safety source was retrieved. The **salmon-aubergine checkerboard** has strong evidence (FDA/EPA list salmon as a "Best Choice" at 2-3 servings a week [S?] ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/eating-fish-what-pregnant-women-and-parents-should-know))), but the kofta's liver hook is sharper for the same pregnancy slot. The checkerboard is the first reserve.

Two judgement calls need the owner's eye. The **savoury crinkle** repeats the scrunched-phyllo shape, so followers who saw the cake will not find it "never seen". It stays in because the shape is the account's single proven winner (142.7x the median, about 500K views, 2.8 shares and 3.5 saves per 1,000 reach [A]), and because its filling, mistake and audience are all new. If the owner wants no repeated shape, move the tea hook to the kofta grid (it has plant iron from lentils) and give pregnancy to the salmon checkerboard ("cutting out fish in pregnancy is the mistake"). That swap gives up the liver hook. The **yogurt bark** loses the notes' 40 g chocolate drizzle so it cannot be read as a second chocolate bark. That costs the chocolate-shell crackle, but the frozen slab still cracks loudly.

### 1. Chicken-crust grid pizza puts 54 g protein in a square

|---|---|

| Ingredients | 500 g cooked chicken breast (poached, shredded fine and squeezed dry; or halal canned chicken); 2 eggs (100 g); 40 g halal-certified parmesan-style hard cheese; garlic powder; oregano. Toppings: 150 g passata, 120 g part-skim mozzarella, 100 g red pepper, 100 g mushrooms, 30 g spinach |

(4) Bake bare at 220 °C for 20-25 min until dry and golden; this is the mistake step. (5) Spread the passata, put one topping per square in a checkerboard and add the mozzarella. (6) Bake 8 min more and cut along the scores |
| Macros per serving [E] | About 390 kcal · 54 g protein · 8 g carbs · 15 g fat · 1.7 g fibre |
| Halal check | Halal-slaughtered chicken, canned included. Mozzarella and the parmesan-style cheese need microbial or halal-certified rennet |
| The mistake and its reason | "Sauce on a raw crust is the mistake." Water at the surface holds the crust near 100 °C, while browning (the Maillard reaction) needs about 140 °C, so a sauced raw crust steams instead of crisping [S] ([Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/845923/the-reason-you-should-pat-steak-dry-before-cooking-it); [Serious Eats via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/juicy-steak-perfect-crust-every-013000071.html)). Evidence: **Moderate**. The sources are about steak, so the chicken-crust application is an inference |
| Benefit line (EN) | "About 54 g protein a serving, with a crust made of chicken instead of flour: one of your protein meals for the day." |
| Benefit line (AR) | «نحو ٥٤ غرامًا من البروتين في الحصة، والقاعدة من الدجاج بدل الطحين: وجبة من وجبات البروتين في يومك.» |
| What not to claim | "Builds muscle fast", "anabolic", "burns fat", any result in kg or weeks, "zero-carb" (it has about 8 g) |

### 2. The savoury crinkle makes tea the villain

| | |
|---|---|
| Audience and problem | Young women told they are low in iron, in homes where tea follows every meal. Serves 6. Pregnancy-safe only when the custard is fully set |
| Ingredients | 250 g phyllo; 500 g spinach; 100 g red lentils (dry); 150 g red pepper, diced; 4 eggs (200 g); 250 g plain pasteurised Greek yogurt; 30 g olive oil; 30 ml lemon juice plus zest; nigella seeds; sumac; 1 g salt |
| Method | (1) Cook the lentils 10-12 min and drain. (2) Wilt the spinach, squeeze it dry and chop it, then mix with the lentils, pepper, zest and nigella. (3) Scrunch each lightly oiled phyllo sheet into a ruffle and stand them upright, packed in rows. (4) Stuff the filling into the folds and bake at 180 °C for 10 min. (5) Pour in the egg-yogurt custard and bake 30-35 min until fully set, with no wobble. (6) Squeeze lemon over. (7) Serve with water, and tea an hour later; this is the mistake step |
| Macros per serving [E] | About 328 kcal · 18 g protein · 39 g carbs · 11 g fat · 4.9 g fibre · about 5.4 mg iron (assumes iron-enriched phyllo; about 1.3 mg less if not) · about 58 mg vitamin C · about 400 mg sodium |
| Halal check | Read the phyllo label: flour, water and oil, and if it contains vinegar, not wine vinegar. Use yogurt with no gelatin |
| The mistake and its reason | "Tea after it is the mistake." Tea polyphenols bind non-haem (plant) iron. Black tea cut iron absorption from a meal by 79-94% in isotope studies [S?] ([Hurrell 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10999016/)). Tea cut it by 64% and coffee by 39% from a hamburger meal, and coffee drunk an hour before the meal had no effect [S] ([Morck 1983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6402915/)). Vitamin C from the lemon and pepper increases non-haem iron absorption, an EFSA-authorised wording [S] ([EFSA](https://efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1226)). Evidence: **Strong** for a single meal. Over a whole diet the effect is smaller [S] ([Cook & Reddy 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11124756/)), and the one-hour gap after a meal is a practical rule, not a tested one. Spinach iron is poorly absorbed [S] ([NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/)), so the lentils carry the iron and the spinach is there for bulk |
| Benefit line (EN) | "Lentils for iron, plus lemon and red pepper, whose vitamin C increases iron absorption. Have your tea an hour later, not with the meal." |
| Benefit line (AR) | «عدس للحديد، مع ليمون وفلفل أحمر فيهما فيتامين C الذي يزيد امتصاص الحديد. اشربوا الشاي بعد ساعة، لا مع الأكل.» |
| What not to claim | "Cures anaemia", "raises haemoglobin", "replaces iron tablets", "fixes tiredness", "spinach is the iron king". Add: "If you think your iron is low, get a blood test; diagnosed anaemia needs your doctor." |

### 3. Honey keeps the dieter's bark from turning to ice

| | |
|---|---|
| Audience and problem | Women in a calorie deficit whose diet breaks at night on sweets. Serves 6 |
| Ingredients | 600 g plain 0%-fat Greek yogurt; 30 g halal-certified vanilla whey (optional); 40 g honey; 200 g strawberries, sliced; 100 g blueberries |
| Method | (1) Whisk the honey (and whey) into the yogurt; this is the mistake step. (2) Spread about 8 mm thick in the lined tray. (3) Press the strawberry slices and blueberries in alternating rows, like tiles. (4) Freeze 3-4 h. (5) Lift and snap straight from the freezer, because bark melts fast [S] ([Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/1864900/frozen-yogurt-bark-snack-ice-cream-tip)) |
| Macros per serving [E] | About 118 kcal · 15 g protein · 14 g carbs · 0.6 g fat · 1.1 g fibre (without whey, about 99 kcal and 11 g protein). Recalculated here from the notes' values after removing the chocolate |
| Halal check | Plain yogurt only, because some flavoured or low-fat yogurts contain gelatin. Halal-certified whey, because some whey comes from cheese made with animal rennet |
| The mistake and its reason | "Yogurt without honey is the mistake." Dissolved sugar lowers the freezing point, so less water turns to ice, and honey's glucose and fructose do more per gram than table sugar [S] ([America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/261-the-best-homemade-frozen-yogurt)). Yogurt is about 88% water, which forms large ice crystals; thin layers freeze faster, with smaller crystals [S] ([The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com/1996387/how-to-prevent-frozen-yogurt-turning-icy/)). The honey costs about 20 kcal a piece [E]. Evidence: **Strong** for the physics; the sources are test kitchens |
| Benefit line (EN) | "About 120 kcal and 15 g protein a piece: a sweet that fits a calorie deficit." |
| Benefit line (AR) | «نحو ١٢٠ سعرة حرارية و١٥ غرامًا من البروتين في القطعة: حلى يناسب العجز في السعرات.» |
| What not to claim | "Fat-burning", "lose X kg", "eat as much as you want", "sugar-free" or "guilt-free" (WHO counts honey as a free sugar [S], [WHO](https://who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children)). Not for children under 2, for whom the AHA advises no added sugars [S] ([The Hospitalist](https://community.the-hospitalist.org/content/aha-limit-childrens-added-sugar-intake-25-g/day)) |

### 5. Whipped whites lighten the post-training oats

| | |
|---|---|
| Audience and problem | Women who train before work and need carbohydrate and protein afterwards, plus a breakfast that keeps for days. Serves **5** (changed here from the notes' 6, so each serving clears 20 g protein) |
| Ingredients | 200 g rolled oats; 3 bananas (300 g: 1.5 mashed, 1.5 halved lengthwise); 3 eggs, separated; 250 g Greek yogurt; 200 ml skim milk; 30 g halal-certified whey (optional); 1 tsp baking powder; cinnamon; 20 g demerara sugar; 30 g peanut butter; a squeeze of lemon |
| Method | (1) Mix the oats, mashed banana, yolks, yogurt, milk, whey, baking powder and cinnamon; rest 10 min. (2) Whip the whites to soft peaks with the lemon in a clean, grease-free bowl; this is the mistake step. (3) Fold in gently, spread in the tray and lay the banana halves in tight parallel rows. ## Four guardrails apply to every card

The wording rule is the same across all eleven: state what is in the food and what it does in the meal, describe study findings in the past tense with "in small studies", and never promise an outcome for the viewer's condition. In Arabic, that means describing the food («غني بالألياف»، «٣٠ غرام بروتين»، «ملح أقل من الوصفة التقليدية») and never using «يعالج»، «يخفض السكر»، «يشفي» or «بديل الدواء». EU authorised claims show what a legal claim looks like. Barley beta-glucan, for example, needs 3 g a day stated to the consumer and at least 1 g per portion [S] ([Reg 1048/2012](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/1048/introduction/data.xht)). The Saudi SFDA and GSO rules on health claims in social food content were not researched, and that gap matters most for the paid PDF.

The halal checks recur and belong in the PDF's front matter. Five apply: rennet in cottage cheese, mozzarella, feta, akkawi and parmesan-style cheese; gelatin in low-fat or flavoured yogurt; whey from cheese made with animal rennet, so buy halal-certified; wine vinegar, replaced by apple cider or date vinegar; and alcohol in vanilla extract and some infused-oil flavourings. Cream of tartar is replaced by lemon to avoid an unresolved question. No recipe uses pork, alcohol or mirin.

Three safety lines travel with specific cards. Every egg in a pregnancy-adjacent recipe must be fully set, because regional eggs are not covered by the UK Lion-scheme exemption [S] ([NI Direct](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/foods-avoid-pregnancy)). Home-made garlic oil should be made fresh and used the same day until a storage-safety source is found. Anyone on insulin or sulfonylureas should not change dosing based on the fattoush order trick.

Production is the binding constraint. Eleven untested recipes in eleven days means test cooks must run ahead of filming. The tray-fill time for standing okra, phyllo and sweet potato slices is unmeasured. The house rule is that a recipe not in the book is saved to `content-studio/book-additions/` and approved by the owner before any video prompt is written [A], so each card should be filed there as its test cook passes.

## Conclusion

The best hooks in this set are not the most surprising facts. They are the ones that attack a habit the whole region shares: tea after the meal, liver for iron, salt in the pot, garlic in everything. In those four the strongest evidence and the widest reach point the same way, which is rare in health content and a reason to lead with them in the PDF's marketing. The cook-and-cool "hacks" that dominate glucose content went the other way: the more viral they felt, the weaker the trials, so the set leaves them out.

The second takeaway concerns what the account is missing. Its crinkle-way reels already hold attention (the pasta pie lost only 31% at the skip [A]), but shares were zero. The parent-targeted trays (fattoush, oat slab, okra) are designed for a daughter to send. That makes the last four days a share experiment, not filler. Watch shares per 1,000 views on days 6, 10 and 11 against the gym days. The Arabic healthy-recipe space has no creator above a million followers [S?] ([Arab News](https://arab.news/c6468)), so if the shares come, a macro-labelled, claim-careful, halal-checked PDF has room to own the space.
