# Arabic-speaking and MENA food creators who grew fast on Reels and TikTok (2023-2026), and what Arabic food audiences respond to

Method note for the report writer: page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked by this session's network allowlist (en.wikipedia.org, almodon.com and 24.ae all failed to resolve), so every finding below comes from web-search result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Follower counts drift and third-party trackers disagree. Each count is given with its source and date where known, and conflicts are flagged. Material older than 2023 is marked [pre-2023].

## 1. Who are the notable fast-growing Arabic food creators (2023-2026), what do their reels look like, and how big are they?

### Takeaway
The biggest Arab food accounts of 2023-2026 are Levantine (Lebanese, Syrian and Palestinian). They win with a repeatable signature: a costume and music formula (Abir El Saghir), a spoken catchphrase (Hussein Fayyad's "Let's make"), or a named seasonal series (The Golden Balance's "Ramadan Chronicles"). Gulf audiences lean towards restaurant-review and mukbang personalities on YouTube and Snapchat. Several Gaza cooks grew to the millions during the 2023-2025 war through survival-cooking stories. I found few reliable numbers for Jordanian, Iraqi or Maghrebi creators.

### Inferences
- The formats that recur among the top Arab accounts are:
  - A fixed visual or verbal signature that viewers can copy or meme (Abir's costumes; Fayyad's "Let's make", which fans imitate).
  - Heritage dishes presented with a twist (mansaf sushi; Middle Eastern dishes "with global flavours").
  - A named Ramadan series (Golden Balance; mxriyum's "Ramadan Series").
- A creator-specific catchphrase that fans imitate seems to be a growth engine in Arabic TikTok (Fayyad). This fits the regional appetite for comedy and parody.
- Gaza creators show that context and story (a child cooking under siege, a clay oven on a rooftop) drove pan-Arab and global growth more than recipe novelty did. The growth was tied to news events and is not a repeatable tactic.
- Platform mix differs by country. Saudi audiences skew to YouTube and Snapchat, so a Jordan-based Reels creator targeting KSA may need cross-posting.

### Gaps
- No verified Jordanian food creator with documented fast growth in 2023-2026 was found. Jordan-specific creators need manual checking on Instagram and TikTok.
- No reliable follower counts for Iraqi or Maghrebi creators, or for Hussein Fayyad's TikTok follower total (only likes).
- No time-series growth data (e.g. Social Blade) could be retrieved because page fetching was blocked.
- Face-on-camera vs hands-only, and dialect voiceover vs music, are described for only a few creators (Abir: music and outfits; Fayyad: spoken catchphrase). Other creators' formats were not documented in the sources.
- High-protein or fitness Arabic food creators: only one small example was found (Nosiba Fayed, a TikTok creator posting high-protein bakes with calorie counts and a school/university lunchbox series), with no follower numbers — [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@nosiba_fayed/video/7490906654418930952). No Arabic "comedy cook" with verified numbers was found.

## 2. Do Arabic-language dialect voiceovers outperform silent or English content for Arab audiences?

### Takeaway
There is no controlled evidence that dialect voiceover beats silent or English content in food reels. Weak evidence leans towards Arabic-led or bilingual content. The single largest Arab food account (Abir El Saghir) grew mostly on music and visuals with no recipe narration, while the 2025 TikTok MENA food winner (Fayyad) is voice- and catchphrase-led. Both routes demonstrably work.

### Cited Findings
- An academic study of #halal posts on Instagram (2017) found that Arabic-language posts got more likes on average than English or Indonesian ones. It does not separate dialect from MSA, is not food-reel specific, and predates this period [pre-2023] — [arXiv 1711.07208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.07208)
- Marketing guides claim Arabic-first posts get 15-25% higher engagement than English in KSA, and bilingual Arabic-English posts in the UAE get about 40-43% more than English-only. These are vendor claims with no published method — [Hovi guide 2026](https://thehovi.com/blog/industry-guides/social-media-marketing-gcc-mena-guide-2026); [Positively Adam](https://positivelyadam.com/blog/arabic-or-english-social-media-uae.html)
- A sociolinguistic study of Arab influencers found that English is used to reach wider audiences while keeping cultural identity — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/83190561/Arab_Influencers_and_Social_Media_A_Sociolinguistic_Study_of_a_New_Culture). A Tunisian study found users preferred English for sharing posts, while mixed-language use helped engagement with diverse audiences [pre-2023] — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348056253_Case_Study_of_Language_Preferences_in_Social_Media_of_Tunisia)
- Abir El Saghir's videos are music-driven, with outfits and visuals and no spoken recipe. She described her output as "vibes" more than recipes — [Yahoo News / Reuters](https://www.yahoo.com/news/tiktok-chef-shares-recipes-around-165907711.html)
- Hussein Fayyad's identity rests on a spoken English catchphrase ("Let's make") inside Arabic-dialect content, and he won the 2025 TikTok MENA food award — [Megaphone](https://megaphone.news/cards/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%AD%D8%B3%D9%8A%D9%86-%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6-%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B2%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D9%8A%D9%83-%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%83-%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%81%D8%B6%D9%84-%D8%B5%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%89-%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%AE)

### Inferences
- Silent or music-only reels travel across dialect borders and to non-Arab viewers (Abir's global audience). A dialect voice builds intimacy and "personality" (Fayyad's fan imitations). A reasonable reading is that dialect voice raises loyalty and shares within the home region, while silent or visual-first content maximises reach.
- Mixing in English words (code-switching, as in "Let's make") is normal in Levantine creator speech and does not appear to hurt.

### Gaps
- No A/B test, platform data or peer-reviewed study compares dialect voiceover against silent or English food reels for Arab audiences.
- No data on whether a Jordanian or Levantine dialect travels well to Gulf or Maghrebi viewers.

## 3. What dishes and topics trend among Arab audiences, and what triggers sharing?

### Takeaway
Ramadan is the single biggest food-content moment in the region: creators report 2-3x engagement, and TikTok says most users watch more cooking content then. Heritage dishes (maqluba, msakhan, baklava, flatbread) and heritage "with a twist" do well. Identity and ownership questions (e.g. "whose hummus") and "how not to make" framing trigger comment debate. WhatsApp is near-universal, which supports private sharing to family groups, but I found no food-specific sharing data.

### Cited Findings
- A TikTok study cited by Arab News found that 85% of users spent more time watching cooking videos during Ramadan, and 57% were inspired by creators to try new household products then — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2285301/amp)
- In Ramadan 2020, TikTok saw a midday spike, with more women opening the app for short cooking videos. Cooking was one of four lead categories, and hashtags such as #RamadanFood, #SahoorTime and #CookFromHome grew [pre-2023] — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1831612/media); [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/lets-talk-about-ramadan-tiktok-sheds-light-on-its-community-trends-in-mena/)
- TikTok Ramadan 2021 campaigns included #GuessTheDish (254M views) and #CookWithTheStars (128M views) [pre-2023] — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/ramadan-2021-a-month-filled-with-support-food-and-tv-shows-on-tiktok/)
- Gaza food blogger Al-Kahlout (about 250K followers) said followers and interaction rise 2-3x during Ramadan. Arabic cooking channel Fatafeat reported an 88% month-on-month rise in Facebook engagement and 29.7M video views in Ramadan. Both figures are self-reported, about 2022 — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/arab-food-bloggers-enjoy-huge-social-media-following-during-ramadan-2061276)

- mxriyum wrote during the Gaza war that "it has felt really weird and uncomfortable to post recipes recently, but I know you all love learning Palestinian recipes". Heritage cooking became a form of solidarity content — [TikTok @mxriyum](https://www.tiktok.com/@mxriyum/video/7312591827943673118?lang=en)
- Platform context: in the Arab Youth Survey 2024 (3,600 people aged 18-24 in 18 Arab states), the platforms rated "most important" were Facebook 18%, Instagram 17%, WhatsApp 16%, YouTube 13%, TikTok 12% and Snapchat 11%. 61% get news from social media, and 74% say they struggle to disconnect — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/majority-of-arab-youth-say-social-media-addiction-is-leading-to-decline-in-mental-well-being-15th-annual-asdaa-bcw-arab-youth-survey-301952104.html); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/mena/majority-of-arab-youth-say-social-media-leads-to-decline-in-mental-health). An earlier edition found WhatsApp used daily by 82%, Facebook by 72% and Instagram by 61% — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/a-key-trend-in-latest-arab-youth-survey-is-decline-in-news-consumption-2167336)

### Inferences
- A Ramadan content calendar (iftar, suhoor, sweets, a numbered daily series) is likely the highest-leverage season for an Arabic food account. It should be prepared before Ramadan starts, because engagement roughly doubles to triples.
- "The right way / how NOT to make" framing on contested heritage dishes (maqluba, mansaf, hummus) invites comment debate, which the algorithm rewards. This is inferred from the captions found; I have no measured lift.
- High daily WhatsApp use suggests that "send this to your mum or family group" style CTAs fit the region. No direct data was found on food reels being shared via WhatsApp.

### Gaps
- No data on budget family meals, or on specific nostalgia dishes (mansaf, shawarma) as measured performers. Only anecdotal examples were found.
- No measured data on tagging or sharing behaviour for Arab food content (share rates, WhatsApp forwards).
- No 2026 Ramadan figures were found.
- No documented "right way" comment-war case study for mansaf or maqluba with numbers.

## 4. Bilingual (Arabic + English) food accounts: how they handle language, and does it help or hurt growth?

### Takeaway
The fastest-growing diaspora Arab food accounts (Golden Balance, Feelgoodfoodie, mxriyum) work mainly in English with Arabic dish names and heritage framing. Region-based winners (Fayyad) speak dialect but use English hooks or catchphrases. Evidence that bilingual content beats English-only comes only from vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
- **Feelgoodfoodie, Yumna Jawad (Lebanese-American, grew up in Dearborn, Michigan).** Her content is English-led. Early growth came from tagging other food accounts that tagged her back. She said she gained about 1M Instagram followers in one year by following what the platform was pushing (Reels), and YouTube Shorts took her to 1M subscribers. She self-reports about 11M followers across platforms — [Food Blogger Pro podcast](https://www.foodbloggerpro.com/podcast/yumna-jawad-instagram-tiktok/); [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/road-to-1-million-with-feel-good-foodie/); [WZZM13](https://www.wzzm13.com/article/news/local/culinary-journey-inspires-over-10-million-followers/69-16fa075f-0382-4400-8ec9-a595e8468954); [Eat Blog Talk](https://eatblogtalk.com/feelgoodfoodie2/)
- CreatorDB (late Sept / early Oct 2026) gives Feelgoodfoodie about 4.8M Instagram, 1.7M YouTube and 2.1M TikTok, about 8.6M combined. Instagram is flat to slightly down (about -7K over 30 days). This conflicts with her self-reported 10-11M — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/feelgoodfoodie/)
- The Golden Balance (Syrian-American) is English-led, makes Middle Eastern home cooking and runs the "Ramadan Chronicles" series. He grew from 5.1M to about 8.6M TikTok followers — [The National 2023](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/food/2023/04/11/tiktok-food-creators-rustling-up-ramadan-recipes/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Alzahabi)
- mxriyum is English-language, with Palestinian dish names (msakhan, khubiz taboon) and full written recipes on her blog, linked in her bio — [TikTok @mxriyum](https://www.tiktok.com/@mxriyum/video/7210928720364899630?lang=en)
- Research on Arab influencers says English widens reach while the creator keeps an Arab cultural identity — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/83190561/Arab_Influencers_and_Social_Media_A_Sociolinguistic_Study_of_a_New_Culture)
- Vendor claim: bilingual Arabic-English posts in the UAE get about 40-43% higher engagement than English-only (unverified) — [Positively Adam](https://positivelyadam.com/blog/arabic-or-english-social-media-uae.html)

### Inferences
- Two working models exist:
  - English voice and text with Arabic dish names and cultural framing. This reaches both the diaspora and non-Arab viewers, and it is how the largest diaspora accounts grew.
  - Dialect voice with English hook words or catchphrases. This is how the region's award winner works.
- Dropping Arabic dish names, or "neutralising" the heritage, seems to cost the identity hook. Fully translating everything has not been shown to be necessary.

### Gaps
- No source measured bilingual on-screen text (Arabic + English captions on the same reel) against single-language reels.
- No case found of a creator who switched language and reported the effect on growth.

## 5. Halal and Muslim food creators in the West who grew fast, and what they did

### Takeaway
Halal-specific review accounts in the West are mostly mid-sized (tens to low hundreds of thousands). The large Muslim and Arab diaspora food accounts grew as general food creators with heritage identity (Golden Balance, Feelgoodfoodie, mxriyum), not as "halal reviewers". The only halal-branded account near 1M is Malaysian.

### Cited Findings
- **Muslim Foodies (New York).** About 86.9K Instagram and 90.4K TikTok followers, and about 370 NYC-area halal restaurants reviewed since 2017 — [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/articles/halal-restaurants-in-new-york)
- **HoodieCooks, Mahad Omar (Minneapolis, Somali-American).** Halal and Somali food reviews, cooking tutorials and game-show-style challenges, with 73K+ TikTok followers. The article is roughly four years old [pre-2023] — [BLCK Press](https://www.blckpress.com/post/hoodiecooks-celebrates-halal-somali-food-on-tiktok)
- **UK accounts.** Halal Food Reviews (@halalfood.reviews) has about 40.3K TikTok followers and 688.8K likes — [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@halalfood.reviews?lang=en). "Akhi" (Manchester) shows 67K Instagram and 120K TikTok in a directory listing (self-reported, unverified) — [Joli UK](https://joliapp.com/united-kingdom/food-influencers/). Other UK accounts include @halalfoodguy (London, 10+ years of reviews) and @myhalalfooddiary, with no counts found — [TikTok Halal Food Guy](https://www.tiktok.com/@halalfoodguy?lang=en); [TikTok My Halal Food Diary](https://www.tiktok.com/@myhalalfooddiary?lang=en)
- **Europe (2026).** Hyphen Online lists halal food influencers including an Amsterdam-born creator (about 30K Instagram, 120K+ TikTok) and a Spanish account (about 50K Instagram, about 400K TikTok) — [Hyphen Online, Aug 2026](https://hyphenonline.com/2026/08/18/halal-food-influencers-muslim-travellers-europe-oompie-fais-italian-revert/)
- **Malaysia (not Western, for scale).** Halalfoodie has about 980K TikTok followers (aggregator figure) — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/halalfoodie/)
- **Malak @lakieats** (Saudi, in LA) built 46.9M TikTok likes on "super honest" restaurant reviews, an honesty-as-hook format — [Cosmopolitan ME](https://www.cosmopolitanme.com/celebs/middle-eastern-tiktokers-to-follow)
- Ramadan is also a hook for non-Muslim creators in Muslim-majority markets (fasting challenges, bazaar reviews) — [Malay Mail 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/life/2025/03/20/food-and-firsts-how-non-muslim-influencers-like-mekyun-ceddyornot-explore-ramadan-with-fasting-challenges-and-bazaar-reviews-video/169374)

### Inferences
- "Halal" works as a trust filter (where to eat, what is safe to cook) rather than a growth hook in itself. The big diaspora winners lead with flavour, personality and heritage, and simply keep everything halal.
- Review formats grow through local utility (a city's halal spots) and a strong persona (blunt honesty, game-show challenges). Recipe formats grow through series and seasonal hooks (Ramadan).

### Gaps
- No halal-only reviewer in the US, UK or Europe with a verified 1M+ following was found.
- No documented growth timelines or tactics (from interviews) for halal reviewers.
- Fetching full pages (Hyphen Online, Atlas Obscura) was blocked, so creator names and handles in those lists are incomplete.
