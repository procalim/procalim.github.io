## 1. Which food creators grew fastest in 2024-2026, and what formats did they use?

### Inferences
- The clearest 2024-2026 growth stories share a repeatable, nameable unit: "the cucumber", "Fast Food Secrets Club", "high-protein X with macros", "50 ways to use Y", "African Food Tourist". Viewers can predict and request the next episode. For a solo creator, a fixed frame with one swappable variable seems more copyable than one-off recipes.
- Jalal's model has three parts: a macro number in the hook, a free recipe as the lead magnet, and a paid recipe library. It is the most directly copyable business structure for a small recipe creator. It fits halal and home cooking without changes.
- Spectacle/list titles (e.g., "50 Ways To Use Chocolate") deliver the biggest single-video spikes. They need a production budget, so a solo creator would scale the idea down, e.g., "3 ways to use one ingredient".

## 2. Voiceover vs silent: is there evidence narrated food reels outperform silent ASMR in completion, shares or follows (2025-2026)?

### Takeaway
I found no head-to-head 2025-2026 dataset comparing voiceover and silent/ASMR food reels on completion, shares or follows. The evidence is anecdotal on both sides. Several of the biggest personality-led breakouts use the creator's voice (Logan Moffitt's spoken catchphrase). Food-sound ASMR is credited by at least one chef with better performance.

### Cited Findings
- The Globe and Mail (February 2025) reported that a chef's videos with more prominent ASMR sounds (sizzle, crunch) performed better with his audience. This is a single-creator anecdote, not a dataset. — [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/life/food-and-wine/article-food-videos-asmr-social-media-cooking/)
- Practitioner guides recommend voiceover only when the visuals need explaining. They pair it with on-screen ingredient and quantity text at each step. This is advice, not data. — [Filmora](https://filmora.wondershare.com/vlog-tips/instagram-food-reel-ideas.html)
- A food-creator case-study blog concludes "Relatable speech isn't enough — visual context matters more". The food and its context must be on screen while the first words are spoken. — [Foodies Only](https://foodiesonly.in/blogs/viral-hook-videos-and-food-storytelling-master-video-hooks-in-the-first-3-seconds)
- The same blog mentions a 2025 academic study of sponsored food vlogs. It found that narrative attributes influence viewer engagement by triggering emotional states. The primary study was not retrieved. — [Foodies Only](https://foodiesonly.in/blogs/viral-hook-videos-and-food-storytelling-master-video-hooks-in-the-first-3-seconds)
- Logan Moffitt's breakout format is built on a spoken first line ("Sometimes you need to eat an entire cucumber…"). — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/consumer-trends/tiktok-cucumber-trend-comes-too-late-for-uk-growers/694860.article)
- A vendor guide markets faceless cooking reels ("Views Up 500K+"). This is unverified vendor material. — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/faceless-instagram-reels-cooking-niche)

### Inferences
- Mosseri says sends matter more for reaching non-followers (see Q4). Plausibly, voiceover helps where the viewer needs a reason to send ("you have to try this"). ASMR helps watch time and rewatches. This is an inference, not measured.
- The two are not exclusive. A defensible structure is real cooking sound (sizzle/crunch) under a short voiceover or on-screen text, with the key line also written as text for viewers watching muted.

### Gaps
- No platform, Meta or third-party study isolating voiceover vs silent/ASMR for food was found.
- An unsourced claim says "#FoodASMR and #OddlySatisfying gain 40% more reach" ("Instagram Reels Data 2025"). It circulated in the vendor results with no traceable study and should not be used.
- The commonly cited "72% watch for stress relief" ASMR claim is also unverifiable.
- I could not retrieve a source in this session describing Nara Smith's or Bayashi's format (voiceover vs silent). The Bayashi Wikipedia page was blocked by network rules.

## 3. Face/personality: how much do an on-camera personality, catchphrase or recurring character matter for follows?

### Takeaway
There is no measured face-vs-faceless follow-conversion data. However, every food creator on TIME's 2025 creator list and the streamer deals is personality-led, and the most-documented 2024-2025 breakout attaches a catchphrase and a nickname to the creator. Mosseri's own statement that likes matter more for followers and sends more for non-followers implies two distinct jobs. Personality converts and retains; a shareable payoff reaches.

### Cited Findings
- TIME100 Creators 2025 food entries were all identity-led: Nick DiGiovanni (chef persona), Keith Lee ("internet's most trusted taste tester") and Chef Abbys ("African Food Tourist"). — [TIME](https://time.com/collections/time100-creators-2025/7299158/nick-digiovanni/); [Because of Them We Can](https://www.becauseofthemwecan.com/keith-lee-wisdom-kaye-more-black-creators-shine-on-times-inaugural-time100-creators-list/); [The Ghana Report](https://www.theghanareport.com/ghanas-chef-abbys-makes-times-100-most-influential-creators-list/)
- Logan Moffitt is publicly known as the "Cucumber Guy" and embraces it. A recurring identity attached to one ingredient produced press coverage and measurable sales effects. — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/logan-moffitt-loves-being-known-130000384.html); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/food-drink/cucumber-shortages-iceland-blamed-tiktok-craze-first-time-experienced-this.amp)
- Personality-led creators monetize via brands. Nara Smith's Reformation campaign became the brand's highest-engaged social campaign of 2025. — [Shorty Awards](https://shortyawards.com/18th/nara-smith-for-reformation)
- Netflix is buying creator cooking shows (Mythical, Nick DiGiovanni), per a 2025 TheWrap headline. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/creator-cooking-shows-streamer-deals-netflix-mythical-nick-digiovanni/)
- Mosseri (22 January 2025) said likes are "slightly more important for connected content" (followers) and sends are "slightly more important for unconnected content" (non-followers). This is reported by secondary trackers; the primary video was not fetched. — [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance); [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach)
- Jalal's account is personal-brand-led ("Jalal"), with an origin story attached. It converts to a paid recipe library. — [jalalsamfit.com](https://jalalsamfit.com/)

### Inferences
- For a small creator, the cheapest form of "personality" is a fixed catchphrase or opening line plus a fixed visual signature (same plate, same final bite, same sign-off). It does not need to be full talking-head. That is what makes Moffitt's format recognizable.
- Faceless or hands-only reels can still earn views, per vendor claims. The documented follow and monetization outcomes in this research all belong to identifiable personas.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study comparing follow rate for face-on-camera vs hands-only food reels in 2025-2026 was found.
- No data was found on catchphrase or recurring-character effects beyond case examples.

## 4. Hooks that work for food in 2025-2026 (first-second visuals, spoken, text, mistake/curiosity, results-first vs process-first)

### Takeaway
Instagram's head named watch time, likes and sends (each "per reach") as the top Reels signals in January 2025, so hooks matter because they protect watch time. The many "first 3 seconds" percentages circulating are mostly unverifiable vendor numbers. The directional consensus is consistent:
- show the food or result immediately;
- put the promise in words, spoken and/or as text;
- keep logos and title cards out of the opening;
- make sure the payoff matches the hook.

The documented food examples use a spoken one-line promise (Moffitt) or a number-led text promise (Jalal's macros).

### Cited Findings
- **Platform signal (best-supported):** On 22 January 2025, Mosseri named watch time, likes and sends as the top three ranking signals. He told creators to track average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. — [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance); [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach); [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- **Contested weighting:** Claims that "sends carry 3-5x the weight of likes" are unsupported. Mosseri's wording was "slightly more important". — [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance)
- **Originality:** Original content is preferred over reposts, according to summaries of official statements. — [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach); [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- **Spoken promise hook (food example):** "Sometimes you need to eat an entire cucumber. Let me show you the best way to do it." — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/consumer-trends/tiktok-cucumber-trend-comes-too-late-for-uk-growers/694860.article)
- **Number/result text hook (food example):** "HIGH PROTEIN LASAGNA! ONLY 465 Calories with 58g of protein per slice". — [Pinterest repost of @jalalsamfit](https://in.pinterest.com/pin/heathy-eating-on-instagram-1-or-2-which-recipe-by-jalalsamfit-would-you-try-1-hig--1102467183773397249/)
- **Curiosity vs instruction:** An analysis of 46,605 TikTok hooks reported that curiosity gaps and urgency statements outperformed direct-address and instructional phrasing. The tool did not tie this to a single URL; it appeared among [mean.ceo hook stats](https://blog.mean.ceo/video-hook-retention-statistics/) and [Green Frog Labs](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/anatomy-of-viral-hook). Treat it as vendor data.
- **Visual vs text-overlay hooks:** A claimed 2025 VidMob Creative Intelligence report says "incomplete visual hooks" averaged 2.4x higher 3-second retention than text-overlay hooks. The primary report was not found; it surfaced in the same hook-statistics results. Treat it as unverified.
- **Branding out of the opening:** Removing logos and title cards from the opening seconds improved retention by 15-30%, per one agency's internal 2024-2025 campaign data. This is agency data, not a peer-reviewed study. — [Percee Digital](https://www.perceedigital.com/insights/video-hooks-that-convert/)
- **Hook-to-payoff mismatch:** One analysis found a video with 96% first-3-second engagement that fell to 13% retention. A strong open does not guarantee completion. — [The Viral Sauce](https://theviralsauce.com/playbooks/hook-engagement-strategy) (attribution per the search summary; verify)
- **Food visual context:** "Relatable speech isn't enough — visual context matters more". — [Foodies Only](https://foodiesonly.in/blogs/viral-hook-videos-and-food-storytelling-master-video-hooks-in-the-first-3-seconds)
- **Unverified figures:** "65% who watch the first 3 seconds stay 10 seconds" (attributed to a 2025 Meta internal study) has no published study behind it. Neither does "viewers decide in about 1.3 seconds" (attributed to a "Meta 2025 Attention Study" of 12M Reels impressions). — [mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/video-hook-retention-statistics/); [Green Frog Labs 2026](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/video-hooks-scroll-stopping-2026)

### Inferences
- **Results-first vs process-first:** No measured comparison was found. Two things favor showing the finished dish or a "money shot" in second 0-1 and then rewinding to the process: the Mosseri signal set (watch time, sends) and the "visual context first" finding. The Moffitt and Jalal hooks both state the outcome up front, either as a promise or as macros.
- For a recipe creator, the copyable hook stack is: the finished food on screen at frame one, a one-line spoken promise, and a text overlay with a concrete number (minutes, grams of protein, cost or ingredient count).

### Gaps
- No food-specific, primary-source measurement of hook types (mistake hooks, "stop doing X", results-first vs process-first) was found for 2025-2026.
- Meta and VidMob primary reports behind the circulating percentages could not be located.

## 5. Length and pacing of viral food reels in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
The only length datapoint found is Socialinsider's (as quoted by search): Reels of 30-60 seconds get the highest reach rate, and reach declines as length rises. Instagram made Reels up to 3 minutes eligible for recommendation (previously 90 seconds), but that is permission, not a performance signal. No food-specific cut-frequency data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Reels between 30 and 60 seconds generate the highest reach rate, "with performance dropping consistently as video length increases". This is not food-specific. — [Socialinsider, Instagram Reels Statistics 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/) (search snippet; page fetch blocked)
- Instagram now lets creators publish Reels up to 3 minutes. Previously only Reels up to 90 seconds were eligible for recommendation; longer ones can now reach non-followers in the Reels tab and Explore. The exact post date was not visible in the snippet, though reported elsewhere as January 2025. — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?)
- The claim that "videos over 3 minutes won't be recommended to non-followers" is unverified (no primary source). — [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach); [Creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm/)
- **Food & beverage Reels averages (Dash Social, July-December 2025):** 126K video views, 80K reach, 835 shares, 423 saves and 0.3% engagement rate. The page's dates were inconsistent per the search summary; verify against the PDF. — [Dash Social Reels benchmarks](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/instagram-reels-performance-benchmarks); [Dash Social F&B benchmark PDF 1H 2025](https://pages.dashsocial.com/hubfs/social-media-benchmark/2025/1H/food-beverage-industry.pdf)
- **Reels engagement benchmarks (brands):** Restaurants & Cafes 0.23% and FMCG-food 0.12%. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/)
- **Rival IQ 2025 (via secondary):** Food & Beverage engagement is 0.40%, and carousels outperformed Reels on engagement for F&B. — [Apaya](https://apaya.com/blog/social-media-benchmarks-instagram); [Improvado](https://improvado.io/blog/social-media-benchmarking) (exact attribution not confirmed)
- **Counter-trend on YouTube:** Babish's 1-2 hour real-time "Cookalongs" launched February 2025. — [Wikipedia: Binging with Babish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binging_with_Babish)

### Inferences
- For Reels, a 30-60 second full recipe is the safest length for reach. A sub-30-second "one-shot" works when the hook is a single visual payoff.
- The F&B shares-to-saves ratio in Dash Social's sample (835 shares vs 423 saves) suggests food reels get shared more than saved. That aligns with Mosseri's emphasis on sends.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 source measured cut frequency or shot length for viral food reels.
- No food-specific completion-rate data was found.
- Average durations of top food reels (e.g., DiGiovanni or Moffitt) were not available.

## 6. Series and recurring formats as a follow driver

### Takeaway
Series are the strongest repeated pattern behind documented food-creator growth. Moffitt's cucumber series ran from about 3M to 7M+ TikTok followers by late 2025. Crowded Kitchen ran from 30K to 2.4M+ Facebook followers with its "better than store-bought" series. Brands showed the same pattern with Crumbl's weekly drops. Rigorous causal data (series vs one-off) does not exist in the sources found.

- **Jordan Howlett's "Fast Food Secrets Club":** a recurring named franchise. 32M+ followers gained across IG and TikTok by November 2024 **[OLDER]**. — [Wikipedia: Jordan Howlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Howlett)

### Inferences
- A series gives non-followers a reason to follow ("I want the next one") that a one-off recipe does not. That is plausibly why series show up in nearly every documented growth case.
- For a small creator, the copyable series template is a fixed title frame, one variable, a numbered or named episode and a consistent end card. Examples: "high-protein [dish] under X calories", "one ingredient, three ways", "Day N of …". Trial Reels let the creator test two hook versions of an episode on non-followers before committing.

## 7. Trending audio vs original sound for food reels

### Takeaway
No credible 2025-2026 Instagram study isolates trending vs original audio. The strongest specific figure is **[OLDER]** 2023 Fanpage Karma data: songs used in more than 100K Reels gave +17% reach and +180% interactions. TikTok-specific late-2025 figures point the same way. On the other side, Instagram's 2025 messaging favors originality, and food creators' own sound (sizzle, voice) is the format's signature. Trending audio appears to help initial reach; original sound and voice build identity. The trade-off rests on commentary, not measurement.

### Cited Findings
- **Fanpage Karma (2023) [OLDER]:** Reels using songs already in more than 100,000 Reels got 17% more reach and 180% more interactions than Reels with less-used songs. The tool did not pin this to a single URL; candidates are [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/trending-audio-instagram-reach/) and [SocialRails](https://socialrails.com/blog/trending-songs-instagram-reels). Verify with Fanpage Karma directly.
- **TikTok (late 2025, not Instagram):** trending sounds lifted reach by 47%, and original audio saw about 22% lower initial reach. — [TTSVibes insights](https://insights.ttsvibes.com/?p=712)
- Using audio other creators use does not reduce reach or trigger a penalty, a claim attributed to Instagram's head but not verified to a primary source. — [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/trending-audio-instagram-reach/)
- An article attributes a "40% increase in initial reach for trending audio" to a 2025 Social Media Today study. The study could not be located, so treat it as unconfirmed. — [InfluenceFlow trending audio guide](https://influenceflow.io/resources/mastering-trending-audio-on-reels-in-2026-your-ultimate-guide-to-going-viral/) (attribution per search summary)

### Inferences
- For recipe reels, a practical hybrid is a low-volume trending track under the creator's own cooking sound and/or voice. It plausibly picks up some of the trending-audio reach lift while keeping the original-sound identity. This is untested in the sources.
- The documented breakouts (Moffitt, Jalal, DiGiovanni) are identified by the creator's own voice or format, not by a trending song.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 Instagram-specific, methodologically transparent comparison of trending vs original audio for food (or any niche) was found.
- No data was found on Arabic-language or Middle East food reels and audio choice.
