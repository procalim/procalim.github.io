# What makes viewers share, save and follow after short-form food content (Instagram Reels / TikTok), 2024-2026

Method note for the report writer: these notes rest on 18 web searches. Direct page fetches failed for socialmediatoday.com, influencermarketinghub.com and keywordseverywhere.com, because those hosts are blocked by this session's network. Most items below therefore come from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not from the full text. Every item is labelled **[MEASURED]** (a study or platform data with a sample), **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** (said by Instagram, Meta or TikTok, not independently measured) or **[OPINION]** (a creator, vendor or marketer view). Older classic research is marked **(pre-2024)**.

## 1. What content traits drive DM sends and shares of food reels?

### Takeaway
Instagram has said that sends (DM shares) are one of its top three ranking signals, and that they count slightly more than likes for reaching non-followers. Research outside food shows people share content that sparks high-arousal emotions (awe, amusement, anger, surprise), that has practical value, or that makes the sharer look good. The few food and diet studies from 2024 point to humour, a narrative or visual-first style, and surprising or contrarian claims. Inaccurate nutrition claims also got more shares, which carries ethical risk. I found no study that measures the effect of "tag someone who…" prompts or of nostalgia on food-reel sends specifically.

### Cited Findings
**Platform signals**
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** In January 2025 Adam Mosseri named three main Reels ranking signals: watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. He did not rank them. Many blogs say watch time comes first, but that is their own framing. — [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026); [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** Mosseri said like rates matter more for posts shown to followers, and send rates matter more for posts shown to non-followers. Secondary sources quote him as saying likes are "slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." — [Social Media Today, "Instagram engagement rates provide insight into reach"](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-engagement-rates-provide-insight-into-reach/) (seen via search summary only; date not confirmed); [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** Earlier Social Media Today coverage of Mosseri said "Shares per Impression" is now, on average, the most important factor for reach. Net Influencer reported Mosseri calling sends per reach "one of the most important things" Instagram weighs. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-insights-into-its-algorithms-creator-monetization/718540/); [Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/?p=27534)
- **[OPINION / UNVERIFIED]** Some 2026 blogs claim a new January 2026 ranking hierarchy. I found no Instagram or Mosseri source for it, and one source says the claim appears in no Instagram source. — [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026); [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri-vs-measured)

**Benchmarks**
- **[MEASURED]** Socialinsider studied 21.3M Instagram posts from 405K brand accounts, October 2025 to March 2026. Reels had the highest share rate at 0.10% of followers, ahead of carousels (0.08%) and images (0.07%). — [Socialinsider Instagram Engagement Report](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)
- **[MEASURED, secondhand]** A Dash Social (formerly Dash Hudson) study of 3,300+ global brands reportedly found an average Reel gets about 1,300 shares and 618 saves on 283,000 views. I could not check the study's year or method. — [Vidico (citing Dash Social)](https://vidico.com/news/instagram-reels-statistics/)

**Classic research on sharing**
- **[MEASURED] (pre-2024, classic)** Berger & Milkman (2012, *Journal of Marketing Research*) analysed about 7,000 New York Times articles and ran lab experiments. Content that sparks high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) is more viral. Content that sparks low-arousal emotions (sadness) is less viral, and positive content beats negative. The effects held after controlling for how surprising, interesting or practically useful the content was. In the lab, the same story was more likely to be shared when it caused more activating emotions such as amusement or anger. — [Berger & Milkman, Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf); [Scientific American summary](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral/)
- **[FRAMEWORK] (pre-2024, 2013 book)** Jonah Berger's STEPPS model lists six drivers of sharing: Social currency (people share what makes them look knowledgeable or cool), Triggers (everyday cues that keep an idea top of mind), Emotion, Public visibility, Practical value (useful information, which also strengthens social bonds) and Stories. Sources are book summaries, not the book itself. — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/contagious-jonah-berger/summary/); [Shortform](https://shortform.com/blog/?p=46309)

**Food and diet studies, 2024-2026**
- **[MEASURED]** A content analysis of 250 diet-related TikTok videos from five popular hashtags (*Proceedings of the Nutrition Society*, May 2024) found humour was the only content feature significantly linked to engagement. — [ResearchGate: Characteristics of popular diet-related TikTok videos](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380393272_Characteristics_of_popular_diet-related_TikTok_videos)
- **[MEASURED]** The #WhatIEatinaDay study (2024/2025) found completely inaccurate nutrition posts drew significantly more likes, comments, shares and saves than accurate ones (p < 0.05). — [PMC: #WhatIEatinaDay](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11901546/)
- **[MEASURED]** A CSCW 2024 study of teenagers in Saudi Arabia and TikTok food videos found narrative style was the only significant predictor of engagement. Purely visual videos significantly outperformed videos that kept their original documentary-style narration, on both shares and comments. Caveats: the summary gives the sample as ages 13-24, and the stated data window (June to December 2024) is hard to square with a November 2024 publication. Check the full text. — [ResearchGate: From Viral Content to Real-Life Cuisine and Beyond](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385664991_From_Viral_Content_to_Real-Life_Cuisine_and_Beyond_Examining_Teenagers'_Interactions_with_TikTok_Food_Videos_and_the_Influence_on_their_Food_Practices)
- **[MEASURED, small sample]** A 2025 *Asian People Journal* study compared 10 Douyin and 10 TikTok videos from the restaurant chain Haidilao. On Douyin, engagement tracked cultural content more closely; the authors tie this to a collectivist audience that responds to cultural heritage and emotional depth. On TikTok, engagement showed no significant link to cultural content. — [Asian People Journal (UniSZA)](https://journal.unisza.edu.my/apj/index.php/apj/article/download/827/396/4035)
- **[MEASURED, preliminary]** A 2026 pilot case study of seafood-restaurant review videos links narrative structure to engagement but cannot show cause and effect. — [Frontiers in Communication 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1770855/full)

### Inferences
- Treat each food reel as content made to be sent. The research suggests three send motives that likely work for food:
  - **Practical value:** cheap, fast, high-protein, few ingredients, a "you need this" hack. The viewer sends it to the person they cook with or for.
  - **Social currency or identity:** "this is how *we* make it," a regional dish, an argument about the "right" way. Sending it says something about the sender.
  - **High-arousal emotion:** a surprising technique, an over-the-top cheese pull, humour or awe.

  Low-arousal emotion (wistful, quiet sadness) is less likely to trigger a send unless it is paired with warmth or pride.
- Sends weigh most for reach to non-followers. So "share-worthy" food reels mainly drive discovery, and likes drive reach among existing followers.
- Controversy and surprise get shares, as the inaccurate-nutrition finding shows. Mild "debate" hooks ("this is the only correct way to…") may work, but under this project's book-faithful and halal rules, a debate has to be about technique or taste, never false claims.
- For a Middle East audience, the Saudi CSCW finding suggests visual-first reels with little narration may share better. This is a single-study signal.
- Benchmark check: by my own arithmetic from the Dash Social figures, about 0.46% of views become shares and about 0.22% become saves. Per follower, Socialinsider's 0.10% shares and 0.04% saves for brands is the floor. These numbers give the owner a rough "good send rate" to compare against.

### Gaps
- I found no published study isolating the effect of "tag someone who…", "send this to…", nostalgia or "you have to try this" framing on food-reel shares. Evidence on these is only creator opinion.
- I found no Meta or Instagram research on why people DM *food* content specifically.
- I found no Socialinsider share or save rate broken out for the food and beverage industry. A roundup quoting "3.50%" for food and beverage Reels does not say which metric it means. — [Improvado](https://improvado.io/blog/social-media-benchmarking)
- The Social Media Today articles holding Mosseri's exact wording could not be fetched, because the host is blocked in this session.

## 2. What drives saves for recipe reels?

### Takeaway
Measured data shows carousels lead on save rate and Reels lead on shares. That fits the idea that people save what they will return to, such as recipes, and send what they want to talk about. Instagram's January 2025 top-three signals did not include saves, so saves look like a secondary reach lever. I found no controlled data on whether an on-screen recipe, steps in the caption or a "save for later" call to action raises save rates for food.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED]** Across brand accounts from October 2025 to March 2026, carousels had the top save rate (0.05% of followers) and Reels had 0.04%. Reels led on shares. — [Socialinsider Instagram Engagement Report](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)
- **[MEASURED]** In full-year 2025, Reels averaged 0.52% engagement against 0.55% for carousels. The data covers 35M posts from 447,613 profiles and comes via a third-party summary of Socialinsider data. — [Socialinsider 2026 benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram); [Socialinsider 2025 PDF](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/instagram_benchmarks_2025.pdf)
- **[MEASURED, secondhand]** In the Dash Social study, the average Reel got 618 saves against about 1,300 shares (283K views), so roughly twice as many shares as saves. — [Vidico (citing Dash Social)](https://vidico.com/news/instagram-reels-statistics/)
- **[MEASURED]** Inaccurate nutrition posts also drew significantly more saves than accurate ones, so saving alone does not mean the content is good. — [PMC: #WhatIEatinaDay](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11901546/)
- **[OPINION / vendor]** Hootsuite says saves and shares are strong signals to the algorithm that content resonates, and that Reels tend to drive more saves than static posts. — [Hootsuite Reels guide](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels/); [Hootsuite engagement guide](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-engagement/)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** Mosseri's January 2025 list of the main signals was watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Saves were not on it. — [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026)
- **[OPINION]** A "typical" food and beverage engagement rate of 1-2.5% is quoted, but the source cites Socialinsider only for the all-industry median. — [EngagementRateCalc](https://engagementratecalc.com/blog/instagram-engagement-rate-benchmarks-by-industry/)

### Inferences
- A recipe reel gets saved because the viewer plans to cook it later. Saves are most likely when the reel signals "full recipe here": quantities on screen or in the caption, a clear ingredient list, or a pinned comment with the steps. This is inferred from the save-versus-share pattern above, not tested.
- Carousels out-save Reels. Pairing a recipe reel with a carousel version, or a downloadable recipe card or PDF, may capture save intent the reel itself loses.
- Because saves are not among Mosseri's named top signals, a save call to action should come second to a send call to action when the goal is reach to non-followers. A save call to action still fits recipe content and builds return visits.

### Gaps
- I found no measured data on how a "save this for later" call to action affects save rate, positively or negatively.
- I found no data comparing a recipe written on screen against one in the caption against one sent by DM for saves.
- I found no food-niche save-rate benchmark from Later, Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Socialinsider or Dash Social.

## 3. What turns viewers into followers?

### Takeaway
The evidence here is thin. Instagram's only hard data is about reach, not follows: creators who used Trial Reels posted more, and most of those who posted more saw more non-follower reach. Food-creator case studies point the same way: a viral recipe, then a run of follow-up videos on the same theme or a series, then stories that convert. One prominent food creator says a single viral reel brings only a few thousand followers and that "follow me" reels "never work". I found no data on pinned reels, bios or grid consistency.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** Trial Reels launched in December 2024. In July 2025 they opened to all public accounts with at least 1,000 followers. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-introduces-wider-access-trial-reels-9493132)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT, internal data]** In a June 12, 2025 post, Mosseri said 40% of creators started posting Reels more often after trying Trial Reels, and 80% of those saw more Reels reach from non-followers. The footnote describes an internal look at more than 400,000 creators, comparing the month after first use with the month before. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-trial-reels/); [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-trial-reels)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** Mosseri said trial reels almost always get less reach than normal reels, and should be compared with each other, not with normal reels. Quotes: "It's going to get less reach, almost always" and "Because it's not going to your followers, you can't compare it to a normal reel." — [Junibloom](https://www.junibloom.com/blog/do-instagram-trial-reels-work)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** On April 2, 2026, Mosseri said trial reels can now be scheduled ahead of time. — [Flowgent](https://flowgent.ai/blog/instagram-trial-reels)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** In January 2025 Mosseri said Instagram would prioritise original content. — [NBC New York / CNBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/instagram-head-mosseri-says-app-will-prioritize-original-content-with-potential-tiktok-ban-days-away/6105247/) (headline-level only)
- **[OPINION]** A trial reel that works shows up first as a spike in profile visits from non-followers. This is a third-party blog, not Mosseri. — [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-trial-reels/)
- **[CASE STUDY, self-reported]** Joy to the Food (Mika Kinney) grew from 1,000 followers to 480,000 in about two years, per Food Blogger Pro. Another episode gives 250,000, recorded at an earlier point. One brownie-batter recipe went viral and she followed it with a whole brownie series. — [Food Blogger Pro: turning followers into traffic](https://www.foodbloggerpro.com/podcast/turn-instagram-followers-into-site-traffic-and-revenue/); [Food Blogger Pro: 1K to 250K](https://www.foodbloggerpro.com/podcast/instagram-growth-strategy/)
- **[CASE STUDY, self-reported]** The same creator said reels aimed at getting followers "just never work", that a viral video's follower gain is "maybe like 3,000 people", and that she converts most people through stories and then reels. She also talks about having high followers but low views. — [Food Blogger Pro](https://www.foodbloggerpro.com/podcast/turn-instagram-followers-into-site-traffic-and-revenue/); [Eat Blog Talk ep. 805](https://eatblogtalk.com/joytothefood4/)
- **[CASE STUDY]** Vegan creator Maya Leinenbach's cauliflower Alfredo reel passed 1 million views, and her followers went from 70,000 to 200,000 within two months. — [Wikipedia: Maya Leinenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Leinenbach)
- **[OPINION]** One blog describes a 40K-follower food creator averaging about 180K views per weekly recipe reel, with a best of 720K. It is presented as a "worked example" and may be illustrative, not real. — [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/dm-automation-for-food-recipe-creators-2026)

### Inferences
- Views alone do not bring follows. A viewer follows when they expect more of the same: a recognisable series ("Part 2", a numbered recipe series), a clear niche (for example "halal recipes from one book"), a consistent person on screen, and a profile that confirms the promise within seconds (bio, pinned reels, highlights). All of this rests on case studies and inference, not measured data.
- When one reel takes off, the pattern seen with the brownie series is to make follow-up videos on that winning theme straight away. This turns one-off viewers into people who expect more.
- Trial Reels are the one officially supported way to test hooks or formats on non-followers. Judge them by profile visits and follows per trial, compared with other trials, not with normal reels.

### Gaps
- I found no measured data on follow rate tied to pinned reels, bio wording, grid consistency, "Part 2" promises or a consistent persona.
- Instagram publishes no benchmark for how many non-follower profile visits become follows.
- I found no MENA-specific food-creator follower case study.

## 4. Do explicit calls to action and comment-to-DM automations help or hurt reach in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
I found no controlled or platform data showing that "comment X for the recipe" automations raise or lower reach. Every claim that they help comes from vendors such as ManyChat. Meta's rules still allow reach to be cut for "engagement bait" (asking people to like, comment, share, tag or vote mainly to gain reach) on Facebook and Instagram. Mosseri has said hacks usually don't work and get shut down. A keyword call to action that delivers real value by DM, such as the full recipe, is a grey area. Plain "tag a friend" or "comment YES" prompts are the riskiest.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT]** Meta's Transparency Center says it may reduce the reach of content on Facebook or Instagram that does not break Community Standards but is problematic or low quality, and it lists engagement bait as one example. — [Meta: Engagement Bait guideline](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/); [Meta: Lowering distribution of problematic content](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content/)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT] (pre-2024, 2017, Facebook)** Facebook's original engagement-bait crackdown covered posts asking people to vote with reactions, like, share, **tag a friend** or comment in order to get more reach. Single posts would be demoted, and pages that repeatedly used bait could lose reach across the page. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1579223); [Social News Desk](https://www.socialnewsdesk.com/blog/facebook-news-feed-update-engagement-bait-will-see-reduced-reach/)
- **[CONFLICT]** One source says Instagram's own rule against recommending engagement bait was removed in 2025. Meta's current guideline page still lists Instagram. — [Aubrium](https://aubrium.com/blog/engagement-bait-policy) contradicted by [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/)
- **[PLATFORM STATEMENT, secondhand]** Mosseri in April 2026: "Generally speaking, those hack-type tactics sometimes work, usually don't, and when we find them, we usually shut them down." — [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach); [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri-vs-measured)
- **[OPINION, unverified attribution]** Creator Lane says Mosseri has repeatedly said that manipulative engagement formats from 2018-2022 (for example "Comment YES if you agree") are now demoted. I could not find a primary Mosseri source for this. — [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri-vs-measured)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** A Social Media Today article titled "Instagram chief debunks popular engagement hack" exists, but its content could not be fetched. A blog says a related October 2026 post quotes Mosseri: "Doing either is not going to affect your reach." It is unclear which two behaviours he meant. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-debunks-popular-engagement-hack/816781/); [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026)
- **[PLATFORM / vendor documentation]** ManyChat can trigger a DM when someone comments a keyword on a post or Reel. It runs through Meta's official API, which permits this. — [ManyChat Help Center](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/14281316989724-Instagram-Post-and-Reel-Comments-trigger); [Kanal](https://getkanal.com/blog/manychat-instagram)
- **[OPINION / vendor, no data]** ManyChat says more engagement tells Instagram to show the post more often. A third-party blog says keyword prompts "spike engagement" and widen reach. Neither cites any measurement. — [ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/enhancing-engagement-with-instagram-automation-a-strategic-guide/); [Brittany Miller Socials](https://www.brittanymillersocials.ca/post/why-i-use-manychat-for-instagram-dm-automation)
- **[PLATFORM limit]** If the same person comments the keyword twice on one post, the automation fires only on the first comment. This is an Instagram API limit. — [SetSmart](https://setsmart.io/blog/manychat-instagram)
- **[OPINION]** Hootsuite advises calls to action that keep viewers engaged with the video rather than sending them off-platform. A call-to-action guide advises asking cold, discovery audiences for saves and shares, not sales. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels/); [CreatorFlow CTA examples](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-call-to-action-examples/)
- **[OPINION]** Blogs citing Mosseri's January 2025 remarks argue that a send call to action fits the ranking signals better than a comment call to action, because sends count more for non-follower reach. — [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026); [UnlockDM](https://www.tryunlockdm.com/blog/instagram-comments-vs-likes-2026)

### Inferences
- The lowest-risk call to action is one tied to something real, like "comment 'recipe' and I'll DM you the full quantities". It is defensible because the comment gets the viewer something. Generic comment farming ("comment YES", "tag 3 friends") matches Meta's engagement-bait definition word for word.
- Because sends are a named ranking signal and comments are not, a natural send prompt is likely worth more than a comment prompt for reach to non-followers. For example, an on-screen "send this to the one who always says 'we have nothing to eat'". This is reasoned from Mosseri's statements, not measured. It should feel like a joke or a real use, not a demand.
- Comment-to-DM funnels still look valuable for *conversion*: they deliver the recipe or PDF and build a DM relationship, and that may support later reach between the creator and those viewers. Any reach boost is unproven. The owner can measure it with Trial Reels by testing the same reel with and without the keyword call to action and comparing reach, sends and follows.

### Gaps
- I found no A/B test, platform statement or independent study on how comment-to-DM automation (ManyChat, ChatPlace) affects Reels reach.
- I found no primary Mosseri quote, 2025-2026, that specifically covers keyword-comment calls to action or DM automation.
- The current Meta engagement-bait page wording was not fetched in full, so it is unclear whether keyword prompts that deliver value count as bait.

## 5. How do emotion and identity (family, culture, home cooking, Ramadan and occasions) shape sharing of food content, especially for Middle East audiences?

### Takeaway
MENA data comes mostly from platforms and agencies, not peer-reviewed studies, but it is consistent. During Ramadan, family time, food spending and video viewing all rise. Food creators see higher engagement. Audiences respond better to personal, nostalgic, home-made content than to polished brand messages. Academic work suggests that in collectivist cultures, cultural-heritage content drives engagement more strongly, and that liking, commenting and sharing are ways people express cultural identity.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM / Ipsos research]** TikTok's 2025 Ramadan playbook for small businesses cites Ipsos's 2024 Ramadan & Eid research. It describes TikTok as a place where users share traditions, family recipes and wholesome moments. It gives food a headline figure of 82%; the exact meaning of that figure could not be confirmed. It suggests food and beverage brands try Ramadan recipes. — [TikTok Ramadan 2025 SMB Playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Ramadan_2025_SMB_Playbook_ENG.pdf)
- **[PLATFORM, internal data] (2022)** Ramadan recipes on TikTok got 564 million views in MENA in 2022. — [Campaign Middle East](https://campaignme.com/lets-talk-about-ramadan-tiktok-sheds-light-on-its-community-trends-in-mena/)
- **[PLATFORM] (pre-2024)** TikTok MENA said much of the #MyFamily content during Ramadan was feel-good vlogs of families celebrating together. #Ramadan_Preps (2021) featured users sharing their preparations for the month. In 2020, more women used the app around noon to look for cooking content. — [Campaign Middle East 2021](https://campaignme.com/ramadan-2021-a-month-filled-with-support-food-and-tv-shows-on-tiktok/); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1831612/media)
- **[MEASURED, survey]** A Trade Desk survey in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Egypt found 88% put family time above everything else during Ramadan and 76% see technology as an integral part of it. Over half expect to spend more on food and beverages. — [CABSAT / The Trade Desk](https://cabsat.com/76-of-middle-eastern-consumers-say-tech-is-key-during-ramadan-the-trade-desk)
- **[MEASURED, Meta study] (2022)** 65% of Ramadan observers spend more time watching videos during Ramadan and Eid. In the UAE and Saudi Arabia, the top creator categories during Ramadan are health, fitness, food and celebrity. — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/ramadan-business-insights-a-guide-to-meaningful-customer-engagement/)
- **[MEASURED / agency report]** Crowd Analyzer's Ramadan 2025 report found lifestyle, food and fashion influencers tend to see more engagement during Ramadan. It also says consumers respond better to content that feels personal and nostalgic than to polished brand messaging. — [Crowd Analyzer Ramadan Report 2025](https://crowdanalyzer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Ramadan-Report-2025-V.01-EN.pdf)
- **[MEASURED / agency report]** DICM's Ramadan 2025 report says food content "exploded", mixing traditional recipes with cooking segments led by influencers. Over 50% of Ramadan viewing in APAC and MENA now happens online, and 71% of consumers feel there are too many ads during Ramadan. — [Episode / DICM](https://episodemag.com/ramadan-2025-dicms-report/)
- **[MEASURED, survey]** YouGov's Ramadan 2025 report (about 1,000 respondents each in the UAE and KSA) found KSA residents prefer dining out with family, while more UAE consumers plan to order food at home. — [YouGov MENA Ramadan Report Jan 2025](https://commercial.yougov.com/rs/464-VHH-988/images/YouGov-MENA-Ramadan-Report-Jan-2025.pdf)
- **[OPINION / agency]** The biggest Ramadan shift is towards short-form content under 20 minutes. — [BYYD](https://www.byyd.me/en/blog/2025/02/ramadan-marketing-mena/)

### Inferences
- For Arab and Middle East viewers, identity and family triggers are probably the strongest send drivers. Examples: "the way mama makes it", a dish tied to a city or country, an iftar or suhoor fix, a dish for an Eid table, or "send this to whoever's cooking iftar tonight". These match the STEPPS ideas of social currency and triggers. Ramadan is a recurring, predictable trigger: the occasion itself reminds people of the content.
- Personal and nostalgic content beats polished content (Crowd Analyzer), and viewers feel ad fatigue (DICM, 71%). Together these argue for a home-kitchen, person-led style over a commercial look during Ramadan. This fits a creator persona built around a recipe book.
- Content timing matters. Viewing rises during Ramadan; noon cooking searches were seen in 2020 data. The KSA and UAE habits differ (dining out with family versus ordering in), so dish choice and framing may need to differ by market.
- Cultural-pride content is likely shared as self-expression ("this is us"). Mild regional rivalry, such as "which country's version is best?", combines identity with high-arousal debate. It should stay playful.

### Gaps

- I found no data comparing nostalgia-led and utility-led food reels on share rate in any market.
- TikTok MENA figures are self-reported and mostly from 2020-2022.
