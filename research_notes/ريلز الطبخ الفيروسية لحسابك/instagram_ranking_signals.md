## 1. Which signals Instagram says matter most for reels, especially for reaching non-followers

### Takeaway
The only on-the-record ranking list from Mosseri remains January 2025: watch time, likes and sends (DM shares) are the top three signals, with sends "slightly more important" than likes for reach to non-followers (likes weigh slightly more for followers). Later 2025-2026 statements (secondary) add originality as a ranking principle and say comments matter less than sends; the new "Skip rate" insights metric measures the share of viewers who leave in the first 3 seconds, and Meta's Reels system card lists "watch less than 3 seconds" as a predicted event.

### Cited Findings
- On 22 January 2025 Adam Mosseri said watch time, likes and sends are the three signals that matter most for ranking, and told creators to look at average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach in Insights (secondary summaries of his video) — [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach); [blckalpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/instagram-algorithm-2026); [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026)
- Mosseri's split (secondary): likes are slightly more important for "connected" reach (people who already follow you) and sends are slightly more important for "unconnected" reach (non-followers). He described the difference as modest ("slightly more important"), not a multiple — [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/); [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach)
- Claims circulating on marketing blogs that sends carry "3-5x" the weight of likes are third-party estimates, not anything Mosseri said; at least one of the summaries above flags this explicitly — [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/); [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026) (exact attribution of the debunk between these two pages not confirmed)
- A claimed "July 2026 update" adding skip rate as a ranking signal circulates on blogs; one summary notes there is no Instagram post, Mosseri statement or reputable coverage behind it. Unverified — [HighStyle](https://www.highstyle.ai/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026); [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach)
- 23 October 2026 (secondary, single cluster of sources, unconfirmed): Mosseri reportedly said comments are valued in the ranking model but are "not the most important part of value", and that Instagram focuses far more on content people send to a friend. Comments were also absent from his January 2025 top-three list — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/adam-mosseri-shares-instagram-algorithm-2026); [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm/)
- A Mosseri ranking video series in 2026 (dated September 2026 by some summaries; dates vary across sources) is described as naming several principles beyond raw engagement, including originality (more distribution to the original creator than to reposters) — [Bazzaal](https://www.bazzaal.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri); [TrueFuture Media](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-ranking-principles-brands-2026); [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-original-content)
- Skip rate as an Insights metric: Social Media Today reported Instagram added retention insights for Reels in August 2025, including a retention chart and replacing "View rate" with "Skip rate". Instagram defines skip rate as the percentage of views from people who skipped the reel during its first 3 seconds, and frames a high skip rate as meaning people did not find the beginning engaging — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/); [marketing4ecommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/retention-and-skip-rate-reels/); conflicting launch date (April 2026 insights update) in [PersonaXpand](https://personaxpand.com/blog/instagram-insights-explained)
- Via the Marketing API, the skip-rate field may come back empty for newer or low-reach reels — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagrams-updated-marketing-api-metrics-work/)
- Meta's Transparency Center system card for Instagram Reels (the "Reels Chaining" card, last updated 11 November 2025) says the system gathers candidate reels and uses input signals such as the reel's length, similarity to other reels and how well it matches content you interact with, feeding prediction models of what you are likely to engage with — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/)
- Third-party readings of that card say its predicted events include: watching less than 3 seconds, commenting, resharing, sharing off Instagram, following the author, using the reel's audio, and watching more of it than 95% of people who watched reels of the same length. The official page text was only partly visible in search, so this list is not fully confirmed — [AI Social Media Now](https://www.aisocialmedianow.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026); [Aubrium](https://aubrium.com/blog/the-minus-sign); [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/)

### Inferences
- For reaching non-followers, the official hierarchy is: hold attention (watch time, not skipping in the first 3 s), then sends per reach, then likes per reach. Follows are a predicted event in Meta's card but Mosseri did not name them in the top three.
- The Meta card's "watched more than 95% of viewers of same-length reels" framing (if accurate) means watch time is judged relative to length, so a short reel is not penalised for low absolute seconds; it is judged on whether people stay relative to other reels of that length.
- For this creator, near-zero sends is the gap most directly tied to non-follower reach according to Mosseri's own words. Skip rate is the first gate: a viewer who skips in 3 s never reaches the point of sending.

### Gaps
- No primary Instagram page was opened to confirm the exact wording of the January 2025 and October 2026 Mosseri statements; both come via blog summaries.
- Instagram has not published numeric weights for any signal. Any "Nx more important" claim is unsupported.
- Whether skip rate is itself a ranking input (as opposed to an insights display of the "watch < 3 s" prediction) is not confirmed by any primary source found.
- The Meta Transparency Center may have a separate "Instagram Reels" card distinct from "Reels Chaining" (the reels shown after you open one); its full prediction list was not retrieved.

## 2. Trial reels: what they are, how they distribute, what has been said

### Takeaway
Trial reels (launched December 2024, older) are ordinary reels with a "Trial" toggle that are shown only to non-followers (Reels tab and Explore) and kept off followers' feeds and the profile grid until the creator shares them or they auto-share after performing well. Eligibility and timing details differ across sources; Mosseri has reportedly said trials almost always get less reach than regular reels, so they should be compared with other trials.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanic: a trial reel is a normal reel posted with the Trial toggle on; Instagram shows it only to non-followers through the Reels feed and Explore, and followers do not see it in their feed or Reels tab — [Postfa.st](https://postfa.st/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/trial-reels-instagram); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagram-trial-reels-work-72-hours/)
- After the trial, the creator can "Share to followers" manually at any time (it then becomes a regular reel), or turn on automatic sharing so it is shared if it performs well; a trial that is never shared stays invisible to followers — [Postfa.st](https://postfa.st/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [Unfollr](https://www.unfollr.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels-guide)
- Evaluation window is reported inconsistently: 72 hours (from the December 2024 launch description), 24-72 hours, or insights shown after about 24 hours; one source says the current Help Center gives no hour figure and says an auto-shared trial goes out when it "performs well soon after you publish" — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagram-trial-reels-work-72-hours/); [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/trial-reels-instagram); [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-trial-reels)
- Eligibility (conflicting): Mosseri reportedly confirmed in July 2025 that trials were available to all creators with 1,000+ followers and a public account; a newer summary of the Help Center says professional accounts need at least 200 followers and personal accounts at least 1,000, both public, and the account must be eligible for recommendations — [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [AI Social Media Now](https://www.aisocialmedianow.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [Creator Tribune](https://creatortribune.com/instagram-trial-reels/)
- April 2026: Mosseri reportedly announced trial reels can be scheduled, built in response to a creator request (secondary) — [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/trial-reels-instagram); [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/what-are-instagram-trial-reels)
- Mosseri reportedly said trial reels "almost always" get less reach than regular reels, so creators should compare trials against other trials, not against regular reels (secondary) — [Creator Tribune](https://creatortribune.com/instagram-trial-reels/); [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-trial-reels)

### Inferences
- At 1.1K followers the creator is above every reported eligibility threshold, so trials should be available.
- Because a trial is shown only to non-followers, its skip rate, watch-to-end and sends are a clean read of "cold audience" performance, which is the audience this creator wants. Comparing hook variants as trial vs trial is the use Mosseri reportedly endorses.
- A trial that is never shared does not dilute the profile grid, so weak tests cost little.

### Gaps
- The current Help Center text on eligibility and auto-share criteria was not opened directly; the 200/1,000 split and the "no hour figure" wording are single-source summaries.
- Instagram has not said what "performs well" means numerically for auto-share.

## 3. Originality rules: unoriginal content, reposts, aggregator penalties

### Takeaway
Since 2024 (older), Instagram's Reels recommendations favour the original creator over reposters and stop recommending aggregator accounts. On or around 30 April 2026 Instagram extended this to photos and carousels: accounts that mostly post content they did not create, without meaningful changes, are no longer recommended to non-followers (including Explore), though followers still see them; recovery requires mostly original posts over 30 days.

### Cited Findings
- 30 April 2026 announcement (via Instagram's Creators blog, as reported): accounts that primarily aggregate or repost others' content without meaningful changes are no longer recommended to people who don't follow them; this extends a 2024 Reels policy to photos and carousels — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/05/01/instagram-debuts-more-original-content-protections-for-creators/); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/social-media/articles/instagram-stop-recommending-accounts-dont-151646266.html)
- What counts as original: light edits such as adding a watermark or a minor crop generally do not qualify; content someone has "materially edited" counts as original, so templates such as memes can still be used if the user adds an element that enhances them — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/instagram-original-content-algorithm-update/)
- Followers still see the affected accounts' posts; the change hits recommendations (including Explore), not follower distribution — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/social-media/articles/instagram-stop-recommending-accounts-dont-151646266.html); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Recovery: "most" of the account's posts, carousels and reels need to be original over a 30-day period to have the decision reversed; publishers with licensing agreements or explicit creator permission are exempt; Instagram suggests resharing others' work via Stories or the repost feature with attribution — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/05/01/instagram-debuts-more-original-content-protections-for-creators/)
- Mosseri's stated aim (secondary): give more distribution to the original creator than to reposters; the rule applied to Reels since 2024 — [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-original-content); [Bazzaal](https://www.bazzaal.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri)
- Widely shared figures such as "original posts get 40-60% more distribution" are unsupported: neither the 2024 nor the 2026 announcement quantified the effect — [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-original-content); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers) (which of these carries the fact-check not confirmed)

### Inferences
- A creator who makes their own videos (including AI-generated ones built from their own prompts and recipes) is on the "original" side of this rule. The risk is reusing other creators' clips, recipe videos or viral audio-plus-footage without material change.
- Reposting one's own reel unchanged is not addressed in the sources found; it is safest to treat a near-identical re-upload as risky for recommendations.

### Gaps
- Instagram's own Creators blog post (April 2026) and the 2024 originality post were not opened; wording is via news coverage.
- No source specifies how Instagram detects "materially edited" or how it treats AI-generated content that closely imitates another creator's video.

## 4. AI-generated content: labels, detection, and reach

### Takeaway
There are two separate things. The post-level "AI info" label is applied by self-disclosure or automatic detection (C2PA/IPTC metadata, invisible watermarks such as SynthID) and has no published reach penalty. The profile-level "AI-generated profile" label (renamed from "AI creator" on 31 August 2026) does carry a stated consequence: profiles featuring an AI-generated person that do not label themselves can be removed from non-follower recommendations. No credible controlled study measuring the reach effect of AI-labelled or AI-looking reels in 2025-2026 was found.

### Cited Findings
- Automatic "AI info" detection relies on C2PA metadata, IPTC tags and invisible watermarks such as Google's SynthID — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Instagram-is-going-after-AI-influencers-that-don-t-admit-they-re-fake.1386510.0.html); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/guides/instagram-ai-content-detection-and-labeling)
- No published Meta policy attaches a reach penalty to the post-level "AI info" label — [Junibloom](https://www.junibloom.com/blog/instagram-ai-labels-what-triggers-them); [iimagined](https://iimagined.ai/blog/instagram-ai-label-rules-2026); [Virallized](https://www.virallized.com/instagram-ai-label)
- Detection is unreliable (reports, not audits): one report says it tagged real photos edited with background-removal tools while missing fully AI-generated Gemini images that carried C2PA and SynthID; another says Instagram's upload recompression strips C2PA, making metadata-based detection inconsistent — [AI Chat Daily](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-security/instagram-s-ai-content-label-flagging-real-photos); [CreatorLane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-ai-creator-label-2026)
- 31 August 2026: Meta renamed the "AI creator" label to "AI-generated profile" and said it will limit the reach of profiles that feature an AI-generated person but do not add the label; such posts stop being recommended to non-followers in Reels and Explore; profiles that add the label proactively see no reach change; Meta acknowledges false flags and offers an appeal — [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/social-media-marketing/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label-reach-limit/); [Miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label-disclosure-guide-2026); [Junibloom](https://www.junibloom.com/blog/instagram-ai-labels-what-triggers-them)
- Mosseri's year-end memo (31 December 2025, a ~20-slide Threads post): "authenticity is becoming infinitely reproducible"; it will be "more practical to fingerprint real media than fake media"; called for clear AI labelling plus capture-level verification with camera makers; suggested the question shifts from "is this real?" to "who shared this?"; coverage says he sees raw, unpolished posts as a way for creators to signal they are real — [Om Malik](https://om.co/2026/01/01/what-is-instagrams-adam-mosseri-really-saying-in-his-year-end-memo/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/adam-mosseri-suggests-highlighting-real-media-rather-than-ai-content-on-social-media/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/ai-slop-won-in-2025-fingerprinting-real-content-might-be-the-answer-in-2026); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/instagram-says-ai-killed-the-curated-feed-now-its-scrambling-to-prove-whats-real)
- A vendor blog claims AI content gets "23-47% lower organic reach" than human-made content, with no cited data; treat as unsupported — [Ootto](https://www.ootto.ai/blog/does-ai-content-get-less-views-on-instagram) / [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/how-to-monetize-ai-instagram-reels-2026-7-methods) (search did not pin which of these two pages carries the claim)
- Ootto's own test of 79 reels made with AI tools (anecdotal, one vendor, not controlled) concluded AI-made reels can get reach — [Ootto](https://www.ootto.ai/blog/does-ai-content-get-less-views-on-instagram)

### Inferences
- For a food account whose videos are AI-generated food footage (not an AI-generated person), the profile-level reach restriction does not obviously apply; it targets accounts presenting an AI person. If AI-generated people (hands, faces, a presenter) appear, the profile label question becomes relevant.
- The "AI info" label itself is not shown to reduce reach. What plausibly matters is the viewer response that ranking already measures (skips, sends). If viewers react to an "AI look" by skipping or not sending, that shows up through the normal signals, not through a label penalty. This is inference; no study isolates it.
- Mosseri's memo signals a direction toward valuing verified "real" capture and account trust; that is a stated outlook, not a ranking rule.

### Gaps
- No independent controlled study (with disclosed sample size) on reach of AI-labelled vs unlabelled reels in 2025-2026 was found.
- Meta's own Help Center/newsroom text for the 31 August 2026 change was not opened; the date and reach wording come from secondary coverage.
- Whether Higgsfield/Seedance outputs carry C2PA or invisible watermarks that trigger the "AI info" label was not researched.
- A claim that Meta's Transparency Center shows the label displayed on "hundreds of millions of reels" in early 2026 appeared in search but could not be confirmed.

## 5. Benchmarks: skip rate, watch percentage / completion, share rate, engagement

### Takeaway
Instagram publishes no target for skip rate or retention. Third-party benchmarks disagree sharply on skip rate (Socialinsider's 2026 study reports averages above 60%; a single-account analysis puts top reels near 28% and the rest near 46%), so the most defensible advice is to compare each reel with the account's own baseline. Completion benchmarks for sub-15 s reels cluster around 65-75% (Dash Social via a secondary source), far above the 17-34% "watched to the end" this creator sees, though the metrics may not be defined identically. No reliable published share-rate benchmark for reels was found.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram publishes no target for skip rate or retention — [Owlstat](https://owlstat.io/blog/instagram-reels-analytics/); [The Viral Sauce](https://theviralsauce.com/playbooks/what-is-a-good-skip-rate-on-instagram-reels)
- Socialinsider 2026 study (reported as ~140,000 business reels, early 2026; secondary readings): skip rate = % of plays where viewers swiped away in the first 3 seconds; averages range from 60.50% for 100K-1M follower brands to 65.50% for the 1-5K follower bracket — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/); [Jeena](https://jeena.tech/blog/instagram-reels-benchmarks-2026)
- Single-account analysis of 260 reels: the most-viewed 10% had a median skip rate of about 28% vs about 46% for the rest (n = 1 account) — [The Viral Sauce](https://theviralsauce.com/playbooks/what-is-a-good-skip-rate-on-instagram-reels)
- The popular "under 30% is good" rule of thumb does not survive real data; compare against your own typical skip rate — [Owlstat](https://owlstat.io/blog/instagram-reels-analytics/)
- Uncited targets from a vendor: under 20% excellent, 20-30% healthy, above 40% means the hook needs work (no dataset given) — [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026); [Virallized](https://www.virallized.com/instagram-skip-rate)
- Completion rate (Dash Social data cited by CreatorHouse, sample size not given in snippet): 74% for 7-15 s reels, 72% for sub-15 s overall, 49% for 30-60 s, 46% for 60 s+ — [CreatorHouse](https://creatorhouse.app/blog/instagram-reel-hook-rate-hold-rate-completion-rate-benchmarks)
- Other third-party completion ranges: 50-65% for 15-30 s reels — [OwlClaw](https://owlclaw.com/benchmarks/instagram-reels-benchmarks/); above ~65% "solid" and above 75% "strong" for under-15 s reels, 40-50% for 30-60 s — [Grow Creator](https://growcreator.pro/blog/instagram-reels-completion-rate-benchmark)
- Metricool 2026 study (~24 million posts, reported second-hand): average reel watch time 8.5 seconds, more than double the year before; reels shared 67% more than in 2025 — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-stats/); [Inmagic](https://inmagic.ai/instagram-reels-statistics/)
- Engagement rate: Socialinsider puts reels at 0.48% average (Q3 2026) — [Socialinsider benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram); its 2025 analysis of 35 million posts had reels at 0.52% (up 4% YoY) and carousels at 0.55% — [Socialinsider reels stats](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/); [blckalpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/reels-vs-carousel-instagram-formats). A cross-platform study of 69 million videos cites 0.45% for reels in 2026 — [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/social-media-engagement-rate-benchmarks). Rival IQ's all-industry median reportedly fell ~17% to 0.30% in 2025 (secondary; primary report not reached) — [Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/instagram-engagement-rate/)

### Inferences
- The creator's 36-44% skip rate is below the Socialinsider averages (60-65%) and close to the "rest of the reels" median (46%) in the single-account study, i.e. roughly normal to good versus third-party data. The first 3 seconds are not the main weakness relative to benchmarks.
- The 17-34% watched-to-end on 8-15 s reels is well below the reported 72-74% sub-15 s completion averages. Caveat: Instagram's "watched to the end" and Dash's "completion rate" may be computed differently (e.g. per play vs per viewer, whether skips are in the denominator), so the gap may be overstated; even so, it points at mid-reel drop-off as a bigger issue than the opening.
- Since Mosseri ties non-follower reach to sends per reach, near-zero sends is the largest gap; there is no published "good" sends-per-reach number to aim at, so the workable target is improvement over the account's own baseline.

### Gaps
- No credible published benchmark for sends/shares per reach or likes per reach on reels was found.
- Primary reports from Socialinsider, Metricool, Rival IQ, Dash Social, Later, Hootsuite and Buffer were not opened (fetching blocked); numbers come from search extracts and secondary citations, with sample sizes only where shown.
- The Socialinsider skip-rate figures (60%+) conflict strongly with single-account data (~28-46%); a definitional difference (plays vs views, inclusion of autoplay impressions) is possible but unconfirmed.
- No food-niche-specific skip, completion or share benchmarks were found.

## 6. Reel length: guidance and data on length vs reach, loops and rewatches

### Takeaway
Since January 2025 Instagram allows reels up to 3 minutes and reportedly recommends reels up to 3 minutes to non-followers (the old 90-second recommendation cap appears outdated). Mosseri said Instagram is "still focused on short form". Third-party data shows completion falls as length rises, while Socialinsider reports the highest reach rate for 30-60 s reels. No primary 2025-2026 statement on loops/rewatches was found.

### Cited Findings
- Mosseri, January 2025 (Threads): "We're now supporting reels up to three minutes long on Instagram. We still are focused on short form video over long form, but hopefully this gives creators a bit more room to be creative." — [Mosseri on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DE-efFqyStv); date given as 18 January 2025 by [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-reels-length-limit)
- Instagram's Help Center reportedly now says reels longer than 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences (so up to 3 minutes can be recommended); the help page reportedly changed in mid-2025 alongside a 20-minute upload limit — [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/instagram-reels-length-limit); [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm/)
- Some sources still say a 90-second cap applies to non-follower recommendations, without citing a current Instagram statement; this appears to be stale — [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/instagram-reels-statistics/); [Zeely](https://zeely.ai/blog/how-long-can-instagram-reels-be/)
- Socialinsider (secondary reading): reels of 30-60 s had the highest average reach rate (5.60%); 45-60 s reels had the highest engagement rate (0.35%) of any length bracket — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/)
- Completion declines with length (Dash Social via CreatorHouse): 74% at 7-15 s, 49% at 30-60 s, 46% at 60 s+ — [CreatorHouse](https://creatorhouse.app/blog/instagram-reel-hook-rate-hold-rate-completion-rate-benchmarks)
- Meta's Reels system card lists reel length as an input signal and (per third-party readings) predicts whether a viewer will watch more than 95% of viewers of same-length reels — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/); [AI Social Media Now](https://www.aisocialmedianow.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026)

### Inferences
- Length itself is not a penalty below 3 minutes; the ranking-relevant question is whether people stay relative to other reels of the same length. An 8-15 s reel with 17-34% reaching the end is underperforming its length class on the third-party numbers, so making reels longer would not by itself fix reach; the Socialinsider reach-rate edge for 30-60 s reels is correlational and may reflect content type.
- Short reels make loops and rewatches easier, but no 2025-2026 source quantified the ranking value of rewatches.

### Gaps
- No primary Instagram/Mosseri statement in 2025-2026 on loops, replays or rewatches as a ranking signal was found.
- The Help Center wording on the 3-minute recommendation limit was not opened directly.
- Socialinsider's length data sample size and methodology were not visible in the extracts.
